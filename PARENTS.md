# Your Kid Has an Art Computer

A guide for parents.

## In one minute
- **What:** a tablet or laptop that does only art. When it turns on, your kid is in a drawing app.
- **Apps:** Krita (drawing and painting), Sweet Home 3D (room and home design), Blender (drawing in 3D), and the Library (lessons and art to look at).
- **No internet for your kid.** No web browser, no videos, no games, no chat, no app store. The apps cannot go online.
- **Lessons:** about 300 short lessons in 7 topics. They start easy and go up to professional level. About 6 lessons a week fills a year.
- **Backup:** your kid's art is saved and backed up. You can see all of it on your phone (optional).
- **Cost:** the device (example: a used Surface Pro 5 with a pen, about $160). Everything else is free.
- **Your part:** about 10 minutes a week. Look at the art and ask about it.

```

└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- The bar at the bottom is the **dock**. Tap a name to switch apps.
- There is no desktop, no files window, and no settings. Your kid cannot install or remove anything.

## The Library
The Library has two buttons and one search box.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Tutorials** | Pick a topic, then the next lesson. Each lesson is one page: what to read, the steps, and what to make. |
| **Inspire** | "I'm Feeling Lucky": one random museum artwork, full screen. Tap **Next**, **Save**, or **Open in Krita**. Or browse the collections. |
| **Search** | Finds lessons, book pages, and artworks by word ("cat", "perspective", "kitchen"). |

### How a lesson works
1. **The idea:** a few sentences in plain words, and a short quote from a real artist or designer.
2. **Look:** which picture on the book page to look at.
3. **Steps:** 8–15 small steps in the app.
4. **Check your work:** 3 questions about the idea.
5. **Make it yours:** do it again with their own subject.
6. **New words:** up to 3, with meanings. They come back in later lessons.

Every lesson says what file it makes, for example `Interior Design/Client Home/Plan.pdf`. Every ~8 lessons there is a bigger **project**.

### Why old books?
Many of the best art teachers wrote 100 years ago. Their books are now free for everyone (public domain). Perspective, anatomy, color, and lettering have not changed. The modern parts come from open sources made for sharing: an open college textbook, the Krita and Blender manuals, and David Revoy's files. Before a book goes in, it is checked for age 13. Unsuitable pages are taken out (for example, nude figure pages in the anatomy book). Some old books have old-fashioned opinions. The lessons skip them.

## Is it safe?
| Worry | How the kit handles it |
|---|---|
| Internet | Your kid's account has **no network at all**. The firewall blocks it. |
| Strangers / chat | No browser, no chat, no social apps, no accounts. |
| Downloads and USB sticks | Blocked. Nothing comes in that the setup adult did not add. |
| Ads, tracking | None. Nothing is sent out about your kid. |
| Content | Every book and image is checked first. Museum images come from collections the setup adult looks at before adding. |
| Breaking it | Your kid cannot change settings or delete apps. The setup adult can fix it, also from far away (optional). |

## Where the art goes
- Everything your kid saves goes into one folder: **Art**.
- **Backup (optional, Tresorit):** everything your kid makes and changes (art, notes, settings, brushes) copies itself to an encrypted online account every few minutes. If the device breaks, a new one gets it all back. (The Library of books and pictures is not copied; it downloads again.)
- **You see everything** on the device (all files, all apps' work) on your phone in the Tresorit app. Your kid knows this. There is no separate "send to Mom" step.
- **Your kid gets their files** on a phone or school laptop with the same Tresorit app, with no adult needed.
- If a file is deleted by mistake, you can get it back from the Tresorit history.

## Talking about the art (through Tresorit)
Two shared folders connect you and your kid:
- **Backup** (kid → you): everything your kid makes and changes on the device. File names show the topic and the lesson number, so you see where they are in the course. A daily page (`Art/Daily/`, inside Backup) lists the lessons done and the files made. Each file keeps its **version history**, so you can see how a piece grew.
- **From Family** (you → kid): put a photo or a note in this folder on your phone. It shows up in their Library. Use it for a drawing prompt ("draw our dog"), a "client" brief ("a poster for Grandma's birthday", "redo the guest room"), or a photo of your feedback.
- Your kid can answer with a note in `Art/Notes/`.
- Your kid knows you can see everything on the device. There is no live screen view.

Details and more ideas: `docs/5-backup-and-share.md`.

## Your 10 minutes a week
Open Backup with your kid (on the device or on your phone). Ask one or two:
- "What did you make this week?"
- "What lesson was that? What was the new idea?"
- "What would you change if you did it again?"
- "Show me one new word you learned."
- "What do you want to make next?"

## When to step in
| You see | Do this |
|---|---|
| Your kid says a lesson is too hard | Skip it or go back 5 lessons. The order is a suggestion, not a rule. |
| Your kid is bored | Try another topic, or Inspire. Let them make their own project. |
| An app does not open or the pen stops working | Restart the device (hold power 10 seconds). If it is still wrong, tell the setup adult. |
| The dock shows ✗ next to Backed up | Tell the setup adult (often the Wi-Fi changed). |
| A book page or image seems wrong for your kid | Tell the setup adult. They remove it in a few minutes. |
| Your kid wants a new app or more images | Ask the setup adult. Nothing is added without them. |
