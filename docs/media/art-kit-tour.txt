What if your kid's computer could only do art?
It turns on straight into Krita. Pick up the pen and draw.
Every drawing saves to their own Art folder.
When they get stuck, the Library is one tap away.
Inside are three hundred lessons, from first lines to full rooms.
Here is a harder one: a room in two-point perspective.
Each lesson says what they will make, how long it takes, and what to do first.
The steps come from real art books, with the original page beside them.
When they finish, Done marks the lesson.
Out of ideas? Inspire deals out paintings from museum collections.
They keep the ones they love.
Any picture opens in Krita, to copy and learn from.
Search finds every lesson on a subject, like cats.
Notes is for ideas, lists, and messages home.
Sweet Home 3D lets them design a room, wall by wall.
Blender adds drawing in 3D.
Switch back, and the drawing is right where they left it.
And you? You see every file they make, in your own backup folder.
There is no internet, no web browser, and only one app at a time.
So it runs well on an old laptop with two gigabytes of memory.
art-kit is free and open source. Get it on GitHub.
