# 006. Choose a Drawing Plane

**You make:** ~/Art/3D Drawing/006-planes.blend
**Builds on:** lesson 005
**Time:** 30 min
**Read:** Blender Manual, Drawing Plane; Viewport Overlays (Grease Pencil)

## The idea
A drawing plane is a flat surface for new strokes. Quote: "Strokes are drawn on the plane determined by the XZ axes (front view)." Front draws on the front plane. Top draws on the top plane. The Canvas overlay shows the plane as a grid. Canvas helps you see where your strokes go.

## Look
Look at the Drawing Plane pop-over in the Draw Mode header. Look at the Canvas option in the Overlays menu.

## Steps
1. Open the file from lesson 005. Stay in Draw Mode.
2. Open the Drawing Plane pop-over in the header.
3. Choose Front (X-Z).
4. Open the Overlays menu in the header.
5. Turn on Canvas under Grease Pencil.
6. Draw a square. It lies flat on the grid.
7. Open the Drawing Plane pop-over. Choose Top (X-Y).
8. Draw a second square. Check that it lies flat on the other grid.
9. Turn Canvas off again to hide the grid.
10. You should see: two squares on two different planes.
11. Save the file as ~/Art/3D Drawing/006-planes.blend.

## Check your work
- Did the Front square and the Top square lie on different planes?
- Did the Canvas grid show the plane?
- Did you save the file?

## Make it yours
Choose Cursor as the drawing plane. Move the 3D cursor and draw one line. See how the plane follows the cursor.

## New words
- **Plane** — a flat surface in 3D space.
- **Canvas** — a grid that shows the drawing plane.
- **Overlay** — a view option that adds extra lines or grids.
