# 015. A landscape in two values

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/015-notan-landscape.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 014  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Composition (Dow), PDF pages 79–81

## The idea
Dow says to start a landscape as a design. Think of tone, not shadows. Choose a view with big and small spaces in a border. Make some spaces black and some white. Flat tone comes first. Shading comes after. "flat relations of tone and color are of first importance" (Dow, PDF 80)

## Look
PDF 81: picture compositions by master artists, reduced to two tones. Look at where the dark masses sit in each one.

## Steps
1. Pick a view in your mind: hills, trees, a lake, sky, a path.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
3. Draw four small borders with the Rectangle Tool. Make them different shapes.
4. In each border, sketch your landscape with the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Keep lines simple.
   You should see: Four small thumbnails of one landscape.
5. Pick the border that gives the best spaces.
6. Make a large new document or a big new layer. Draw your best thumbnail large.
7. Set the foreground color to black. Pick the Fill Tool.
8. Fill some spaces with black, such as trees or a far hill. Leave other spaces white.
9. Try it again on a copy. Use Layer ‣ New ‣ Duplicate Layer or Mask, then fill different spaces.
10. Compare the two. Which has better balance?
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/015-notan-landscape.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you try four borders before you chose?
- Are there big and small spaces?
- Did you put tone before small details?

## Make it yours
- Do a night version, with the sky black.
- Do a notan of a street.

## New words
- **thumbnail** — a small, quick sketch to test an idea.
- **border** — the frame line around a picture.
- **flat tone** — one even dark or light, with no change.
