# 060. Draw a place from memory

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/060-place-from-memory.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 027 and 041  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 250, 252–253; Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF page 124

## The idea
Crane speaks of two visions. The outward vision draws what the eye sees. The inner vision draws what memory keeps. It records the sum of many moments. Norton says memory drawings are more pleasant and freer, since you know the place. Yet Crane warns that close study of real form is still needed. "The inner vision re-creates rather than represents" (Crane, PDF 252)

## Look
Norton PDF 124, the note: why drawing from memory is advised for rooms. Crane PDF 253: good drawing needs study of the outside as well as inner vision.

## Steps
1. Pick a place you know well: your kitchen, your street, or a park path.
2. Close your eyes. Recall where the horizon would be and where the lines go.
3. Open Krita. Make a new document, 4000 by 2600 px. Add a horizon guide.
4. Place the vanishing points. Use one point or two points, as the place needs.
5. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Draw the big shapes from memory.
6. Add three details you remember, such as a window, a chair, or a tree.
   You should see: A place that looks like your place.
7. Add three values with the Fill Tool.
8. Now look at the real place or a photo of it. Do not draw yet.
9. List three things you got wrong. List three things you got right.
10. Make a new layer. Fix one error from your list.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/060-place-from-memory.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you put the horizon at the right height?
- Do the lines go to vanishing points?
- Did you find three errors by looking at the real place?

## Make it yours
- Draw another place from memory next month. Compare.
- Draw a place that you invent, using the same rules.

## New words
- **inner vision** — what your mind keeps and draws from memory.
- **outward vision** — what your eye sees and draws from life.
- **record** — a drawing that keeps a fact.
