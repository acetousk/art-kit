# 039. A book with a cup

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/039-book-and-cup.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 023 and 033  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 61–65

## The idea
Put a book and a cup in one picture. The book's back faces you. The cup stands on or in front of it. You look at the whole group at once, so the center of seeing is in the middle of the group. Keep the cup's base partly visible. The book's ends run to one point on the eye level. "a cylindrical object is always placed so that a part of its base is seen, if only a very small part" (Norton, PDF 64–65)

## Look
PDF 61, Fig. 67: a closed book and an open book. PDF 63, Fig. 70: a book with a cylindrical object. PDF 64, Figs. 71–73: the picture plane and the plan.

## Steps
1. Put a book on a table. Put a cup on it or just in front of it.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 2400 px. Add a horizon guide above the group.
3. Draw the book's back as a true rectangle. Draw its ends to a point on the guide.
4. Draw the cover and the thickness of the pages.
   You should see: A flat book seen from above, with its ends running to a point.
5. Draw the cup's base as an ellipse on the book.
6. Draw the cup's top ellipse. Make it less round than the base.
7. Draw the sides and the handle.
8. Check: can you see a little of the cup's base? If not, move the cup.
9. Draw a table line behind. Keep it light.
10. Make the book edges firm and the cup edges lighter.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/039-book-and-cup.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the book's ends run to one point?
- Is part of the cup's base visible?
- Is the cup's top less round than its base?

## Make it yours
- Draw a stack of books with a mug.
- Draw the same group with the book turned.

## New words
- **center of seeing** — the line from your eye to the middle of the group.
- **base** — the bottom part an object stands on.
- **group** — several objects drawn as one picture.
