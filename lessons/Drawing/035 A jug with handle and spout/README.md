# 035. A jug with handle and spout

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/035-jug.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 034  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 38–41

## The idea
Draw the body first, as if the jug had no handle and no spout. Then find the profile line. It is the line you would see if you cut the jug in half. Use it to place the handle. The spout sits opposite the handle. "it is possible to recognize and suggest the solid rounding surface of the object by every line and touch upon it" (Norton, PDF 41)

## Look
PDF 38, Fig. 30: the cream jug. PDF 39, Figs. 32–34: profile curves and how the side boundary differs. PDF 40, Figs. 35–37: the spout and the foot.

## Steps
1. Pick a mug or a jug with a handle. Place it a little below your eye.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2400 by 3000 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw the body as a cylinder with light lines. Add a bulge if the jug has one.
4. Find the middle of the top ellipse. Draw a line from it through the handle side.
5. Draw a curved profile line down the body from the handle. Make it curve like the side of the jug.
6. Draw the handle. It joins the body along the profile line.
7. Draw the spout opposite the handle. Draw its center line first.
   You should see: The spout and handle line up through the middle of the top ellipse.
8. Draw the foot as two cones. The lower edge is an ellipse.
9. Draw any pattern. Curve the pattern lines to follow the form.
10. Make the nearest parts heavier. Make the far parts lighter.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/035-jug.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the spout opposite the handle?
- Does the pattern curve around the jug?
- Are the nearest lines heavier?

## Make it yours
- Draw a teapot.
- Draw a watering can.

## New words
- **profile line** — the edge shape of a form cut in half.
- **spout** — the pouring part of a jug.
- **foot** — the base a jug stands on.
