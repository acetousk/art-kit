# 043. Fingers as wedges and squares

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/043-fingers.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 042  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Constructive Anatomy (Bridgman), PDF pages 60–61, 63, 65, 67

## The idea
Each finger has three parts. The parts are not placed end to end. Between the parts are step-downs, and a wedge bridges each step. The middle finger is the longest. The back of each finger is a row of wedges and squares. "A series of wedges and squares thus marks the backs of the fingers." (Bridgman, PDF 61)

## Look
PDF 63: fingers and the pad between the thumb and first finger. PDF 65: the mechanism of the first finger. PDF 67: finger bones marked with first and second joints.

## Steps
1. Hold up one finger. Look at it from the side.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw the finger from the side as three blocks in a line. Leave a small step between each.
4. Make the middle block the biggest.
5. Add a wedge at each step to join the blocks.
   You should see: A finger made of squares and wedges.
6. Round the edges of the blocks a little.
7. Draw the fingertip as a wedge. Add the nail.
8. Draw four fingers side by side. Make the middle one the longest.
9. Draw the fingers curling in. Each part turns on the one before it.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/043-fingers.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the middle finger the longest?
- Is there a step between each part of a finger?
- Do the wedges join the parts smoothly?

## Make it yours
- Draw a hand reaching for a button.
- Draw fingers holding a pen.

## New words
- **wedge** — a shape that tapers from thick to thin.
- **joint** — where two parts of a finger meet.
- **segment** — one part of a finger between joints.
