# 023. A one-point box

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/023-one-point-box.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 022  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 54–59

## The idea
Put a book flat on a table, with its back to you. Its back is parallel to your face. Its sides run away from you. They seem to meet at one point on your eye level. The back is drawn in its true shape. This is one-point perspective. "the point toward which its ends appear to converge remains always at the level of the eye" (Norton, PDF 57)

## Look
PDF 54, Fig. 51: the book with its back parallel to the picture plane. PDF 57, Fig. 58: the book above the eye. PDF 58: sketching the book with a string and a pencil.

## Steps
1. Place a book or box on a table, below your eye. Turn one face straight to you.
2. Count the edges you can see. Norton counts seven on a book.
3. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px. Show Rulers and Guides.
4. Drag a horizon guide from the top ruler. Put it above the object.
5. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Draw the front face as a true rectangle.
6. Mark a point on the horizon, a little right of the box. This is the vanishing point.
7. Draw light lines from the corners of the front face toward this point.
8. Judge the depth by eye with your pen. Mark where the back edge goes.
9. Draw the back edge. Draw the vertical edge at the back.
   You should see: A box with a top and one side. The back edge is shorter than the front edge.
10. Pick the Assistant Tool. Add a Vanishing Point on your mark. Check your lines against it.
11. Draw over your light lines with firm ones.
12. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/023-one-point-box.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the front face a true rectangle?
- Do all the sides run to one point on the horizon?
- Is the back edge shorter than the front edge?

## Make it yours
- Draw a book at three heights. Move the horizon each time.
- Draw a loaf of bread in one point.

## New words
- **one-point** — a view with one vanishing point.
- **converge** — come together at one point.
- **edge** — where two faces of a form meet.
