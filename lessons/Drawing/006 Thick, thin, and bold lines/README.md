# 006. Thick, thin, and bold lines

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/006-line-quality.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 005  
**Time:** 35 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 68–71; Composition (Dow), PDF page 64

## The idea
Crane says your line should match your subject. A feather needs a soft, fine line. A shell needs a rough, firm line. Dow adds that widening some lines in a pattern gives a new look of dark and light. Choose thin or bold, and then decide what to stress. "The character of our line should be sympathetic with the character of our subject" (Crane, PDF 68)

## Look
Crane PDF 69–70: the feather, the lily, and the scallop shell each need a different line. Dow PDF 64: line patterns with some lines made wider.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2400 by 1600 px.
2. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool. Pick the Ink Fineliner preset.
3. Draw a feather outline. Use a thin, smooth line.
4. Pick the Ink GPen preset. Press harder and softer as you go.
5. Draw a scallop shell next to the feather. Make rough, firm lines.
   You should see: The shell lines look rougher and heavier than the feather lines.
6. Draw a lily or a leaf. Use a delicate line with a slight quiver.
7. Draw four lines in a row. Make the 2nd and 4th lines wider. Press harder.
8. Fill a small square with a line pattern. Widen two lines. Look at the new dark and light.
9. Choose the one drawing that looks best. Draw a circle around it.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/006-line-quality.kra.

## Check your work
- Does the feather look softer than the shell?
- Did you press harder to make a bold line?
- Does each line match what it draws?

## Make it yours
- Draw a fruit with a thin line, then with a bold line.
- Draw a leaf you find outside or at home.

## New words
- **line quality** — how a line looks: thin, thick, rough, or smooth.
- **emphasis** — making one part stand out.
- **contour** — the edge line of a shape.
