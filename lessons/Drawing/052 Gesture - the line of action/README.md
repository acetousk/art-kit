# 052. Gesture - the line of action

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/052-gesture-line.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 005 and 046  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 28, 31, 37; Constructive Anatomy (Bridgman), PDF pages 38, 41, 45

## The idea
Gesture is the big movement of a pose. Catch it with one line before you draw any part. Crane says a wave-line is the line of movement. Bridgman says a hand, like a figure, has an action side and an inaction side. The action side has the biggest angle. The other side is straight. "In the hand as in the figure there is an action and an inaction side." (Bridgman, PDF 38)

## Look
Crane PDF 31: the wave-line of a bowler's movement. Bridgman PDF 41 and 45: hands in many gestures. Crane PDF 28: oval masses along a line.

## Steps
1. Make a hand pose: pointing, waving, reaching, or gripping. Hold it.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 2400 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw one long curve from the elbow through the wrist to the fingertips. Use one stroke.
   You should see: One line that shows the flow of the pose.
4. Draw the action side as an outer curve. Draw the inaction side as a straighter line.
5. Add two or three ovals along the line. These are the forearm, the palm, and the fingers.
6. Do not add any detail.
7. Draw five more poses on the same canvas. Draw each one small.
8. Look at your six lines. Does each one feel like its pose?
9. Pick one calm pose and one active pose. Circle them.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/052-gesture-line.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you draw the flow line before the details?
- Does the action side have the biggest curve?
- Can someone guess each pose from its line?

## Make it yours
- Draw a gesture of a hand opening a door.
- Draw a hand catching a ball.

## New words
- **gesture** — the main movement of a pose.
- **line of action** — the one line that shows a pose's flow.
- **inaction side** — the straighter side of a pose.
