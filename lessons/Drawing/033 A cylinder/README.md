# 033. A cylinder

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/033-cylinder.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 032  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 30–32, 35

## The idea
A cylinder has two ellipses joined by two straight sides. The bottom ellipse is rounder than the top when both are below your eye. The sides must touch the ellipses at their ends with no corner. If the sides do not join smoothly, the ellipse is the part most likely to be wrong. Plan the drawing in light lines first. "All meetings of boundary lines with ellipses must be tangential." (Norton, PDF 35)

## Look
PDF 31, Fig. 20: marking the end points and a margin. PDF 32, Fig. 21: an ellipse bent too much at the end, so the cylinder would rock. PDF 32, Fig. 22: ellipses inside ellipses. PDF 33, Fig. 23: the plan of a circle.

## Steps
1. Pick a tall can or glass. Place it on a table a little below your eye. Sit far enough away.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2400 by 2400 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw a margin with the Rectangle Tool. Mark the top and bottom points of the can.
4. Mark four points for the top ellipse. Draw the whole ellipse in light lines.
5. Draw the bottom ellipse below it. Make it the same length but rounder.
6. Draw the two side lines. They must join the ellipses smoothly.
   You should see: The sides touch the ellipse ends with no corner.
7. Draw a middle ellipse halfway down. Make it a little rounder than the top.
8. Rub out the hidden back of the bottom ellipse. Leave a light trace.
9. Compare with the can. Does your cylinder rest firmly, or would it rock?
10. Draw a heavy line at the front edge and a lighter one at the back.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/033-cylinder.kra.

## Check your work
- Does the bottom ellipse look rounder than the top?
- Do the sides join the ellipses with no corner?
- Does your cylinder look like it rests on the table?

## Make it yours
- Draw a stack of three cans.
- Draw a round tower.

## New words
- **tangential** — touching a curve at one point without crossing it.
- **boundary** — the outer edge of a form.
- **rendering** — finishing a drawing with good lines and tones.
