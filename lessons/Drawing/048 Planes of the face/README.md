# 048. Planes of the face

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/048-face-planes.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 047  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Constructive Anatomy (Bridgman), PDF pages 126, 128–129, 131

## The idea
The head has three main masses: the cranium, the face, and the jaw. Each has flat planes. The forehead slopes up and back. The sides turn at the temples. Think of the head as a building. The planes are its walls. "The relations of these masses and planes is to the moulding of a head what architecture is to a house." (Bridgman, PDF 128)

## Look
PDF 129: heads with block and plane lines. PDF 131: finished heads in several views. PDF 126: the masses of the head, in text.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 3200 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
2. Draw a head block in three-quarter view. Use your file from lesson 047 as a guide.
3. Draw the forehead plane. It slopes up and back.
4. Draw the temple planes at the sides of the forehead.
5. Draw the cheekbone wedge from the corner of the forehead.
6. Draw the jaw as a wedge under the cheek.
   You should see: A head made of flat planes. It looks like carved wood.
7. Pick a light side. Fill the planes that face the light with a light tone.
8. Fill the other planes with a middle gray and a dark gray.
9. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/048-face-planes.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the planes look flat, like a carving?
- Do the planes facing the light look lighter?
- Can you name the three masses of the head?

## Make it yours
- Draw a head from the side.
- Draw a head turned down with planes.

## New words
- **plane** — a flat surface of a form.
- **cranium** — the part of the head that holds the brain.
- **jaw** — the bone of the lower face.
