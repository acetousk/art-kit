# 045. The wrist and forearm

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/045-wrist-forearm.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 044  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Constructive Anatomy (Bridgman), PDF pages 22–23, 70–71, 79, 83

## The idea
The wrist is like a boat. It tips side to side with the hand. Its width is twice its thickness. There is always a step-down from the back of the arm to the hand. The forearm is a tapering wedge. Twisting happens in the forearm, not in the wrist. "There is always a step-down from the back of the arm, over the wrist, to the hand." (Bridgman, PDF 22)

## Look
PDF 79: forearm and hand masses. PDF 83: the hand turning on the forearm. PDF 70 text: two forearm bones and the turning bone.

## Steps
1. Lay your forearm on the desk, palm down. Then turn it palm up.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2400 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw the forearm as a wedge. Make it wide at the elbow and narrow at the wrist.
4. Draw the wrist as a flat block. Make it twice as wide as it is thick.
5. Add the step-down where the wrist meets the hand.
6. Draw the hand block. Put the thumb on the correct side.
   You should see: The hand sits on the forearm with a clear step.
7. Draw the same forearm turned palm up. Move the thumb to the other side.
8. Draw a third view with the wrist bent up.
9. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/045-wrist-forearm.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the forearm wider at the elbow than the wrist?
- Is the wrist twice as wide as it is thick?
- Did the thumb change sides when you turned the palm?

## Make it yours
- Draw a hand with a bent wrist.
- Draw your forearm resting on a table.

## New words
- **forearm** — the arm between the elbow and the wrist.
- **step-down** — a small drop in the line.
- **taper** — become narrower toward one end.
