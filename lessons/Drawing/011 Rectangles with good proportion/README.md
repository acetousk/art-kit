# 011. Rectangles with good proportion

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/011-rectangles.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 010  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Composition (Dow), PDF pages 48–50

## The idea
A square or circle has a fixed shape. A rectangle can change. You can pick its proportion. Dow says no formula makes good proportion. Use your feeling and judgment. Place one line theme in boxes of the same size, then in boxes of different shapes. "Art resists everything that interferes with free choice and personal decision" (Dow, PDF 48)

## Look
PDF 49: examples of rectangular line design. PDF 50: the bell tower of Florence and the Ca' d'Oro in Venice, both made from simple straight-line spaces.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
2. Pick the Rectangle Tool. Draw four rectangles of the same size in a row.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and the Ink Fineliner preset.
4. Think of a simple theme of vertical and horizontal lines.
5. In each box, draw the theme. Move the lines so the spacing changes each time.
   You should see: Four boxes with the same idea, but different spacing.
6. Draw four more rectangles below. Make them tall, wide, long, and short.
7. Draw the same theme in each. Let the lines change to fit the shape.
8. Look at the eight boxes. Which spacing feels best?
9. Draw a star next to your two best.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/011-rectangles.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you change the spacing in each box?
- Did you also change the shape of the box?
- Did you pick by feeling, not by a rule?

## Make it yours
- Design a bookshelf with straight lines only.
- Draw a door, a window, and a cupboard with good spacing.

## New words
- **proportion** — how one part compares in size with another.
- **variation** — a change in a theme.
- **theme** — the main idea you repeat.
