# 034. The cone principle

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/034-cone-principle.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 033  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 34, 36–37

## The idea
A lamp shade is a cone with the top cut off. When its small end is nearer your eye level, you see more than half way round it. When its small end is farther, you see less than half way round. Norton calls this the cone principle. It shows solidity with outline only. "the expression, by outline merely, of Relief, or Solidity, or Third Dimension" (Norton, PDF 37)

## Look
PDF 36, Fig. 27: an electric lamp shade above the eye. The ellipses get rounder toward the top. PDF 37, Fig. 29: cones held above and below the eye, with the point up and down.

## Steps
1. Pick a lamp shade, a funnel, or a party hat. Hold it above your eye, then below it.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px. Add a horizon guide.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
4. Above the guide, draw a shade with the narrow end up. Make its top ellipse rounder.
5. Draw the two sloping sides. Let them join the ellipses in front of the ellipse ends.
   You should see: You see less than half way round the shade. The narrow end is far from the eye level.
6. Below the guide, draw a hat or cone with its point up. Draw the base ellipse and the point.
7. Draw the two sloping sides from the point to the ellipse.
   You should see: The sides touch the base ellipse behind its ends. You see more than half way round.
8. Compare the two drawings. Say which has its narrow end nearer the eye level.
9. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/034-cone-principle.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the sides of the upper shade join its ellipses in front of the ends?
- Do the sides of the hat join its base ellipse behind the ends?
- Do the ellipses get rounder as they go farther from the eye level?

## Make it yours
- Draw a party hat above the eye and below the eye.
- Draw a trumpet-shaped flower.

## New words
- **cone principle** — how much of a cone's round you see.
- **apex** — the point of a cone.
- **flaring** — spreading out wider.
