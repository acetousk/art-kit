# 016. Three values

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/016-three-values.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 015  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Composition (Dow), PDF pages 92–93

## The idea
Add a middle gray between black and white. Now you have three values. Dow says the value of a mass is how light or dark it is next to its neighbors. Keep each tone flat. That makes it easy to judge the value of a whole space. Pick the middle gray so that it is halfway between black and white. "the value of a mass is its degree of light or dark in relation to its neighbors" (Dow, PDF 92)

## Look
PDF 93: a scale of three grays shown as light, medium, and dark, with white and black.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
2. Draw a row of three squares with the Rectangle Tool.
3. Fill the first with white and the last with black. Use the Fill Tool.
4. Click Settings ‣ Dockers ‣ Advanced Color Selector. Pick a gray halfway between black and white.
5. Fill the middle square with that gray.
   You should see: A scale of three: white, gray, black.
6. Draw the landscape from lesson 015 again in this document. Use the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
7. Make a new layer under the lines. Fill the sky with white.
8. Fill hills and trees with the middle gray and black.
9. Keep every tone flat. No shading.
10. Use the Color Sampler Tool to check that you used the same gray each time.
11. Make a second copy. Fill it a different way.
12. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/016-three-values.kra.

## Check your work
- Is your middle gray halfway between black and white?
- Are all tones flat?
- Did you use the same gray each time?

## Make it yours
- Try three values on a still life.
- Make a three-value version of a cartoon.

## New words
- **scale** — a row of values from light to dark.
- **middle value** — the gray between light and dark.
- **flat wash** — an even tone with no change.
