# 009. Measure with your eye

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/009-measure.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 004  
**Time:** 30 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 22–25

## The idea
Before you draw, learn to measure with your eye. Hold a pen at arm's length and close one eye. Slide your thumb to mark the width of an object. Turn the pen to compare it with the height. Norton asks you to guess first, then check. Keep the pen flat, as if it slides on a pane of glass. "What is now sought for is that which the eye really sees as the width, not what the mind knows it to be." (Norton, PDF 25)

## Look
PDF 22, Fig. 7: measuring a book on the pencil. PDF 23, Fig. 8: a window used as a picture plane. PDF 25, Figs. 10 and 11: the pencil turns from upright to flat.

## Steps
1. Put a book on a table, upright, in front of you. Sit about two arm lengths away.
2. Close one eye. Hold a pen out at arm's length. Keep it flat, like it slides on glass.
3. Slide your thumb to mark the width of the book on the pen.
4. Turn the pen to the upright. Compare that mark with the height.
5. Write your guess, such as width = 3/4 of height.
6. Turn the book away a little. Measure the width again. Write the new guess.
7. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2000 by 1500 px.
8. Pick the Rectangle Tool. Draw a rectangle with your first guess.
9. Pick the Measure Tool. Drag across the width. Read the length in the Tool Options docker.
10. Drag the Measure Tool along the height. Compare the two numbers with your guess.
   You should see: Your guess was close, or you see how far off it was.
11. Draw a second, narrower rectangle for the turned book.
12. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/009-measure.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you close one eye and hold the pen flat?
- Did you guess before you measured?
- Does the turned book look narrower than the first one?

## Make it yours
- Measure a door open and shut. See how the width changes.
- Measure the height of a cup against its width.

## New words
- **picture plane** — an imaginary pane of glass between you and the scene.
- **foreshortened** — made look shorter because it points toward you.
- **proportion** — how one size compares with another.
