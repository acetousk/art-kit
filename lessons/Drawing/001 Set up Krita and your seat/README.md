# 001. Set up Krita and your seat

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/001-setup.kra  
**Builds on:** nothing  
**Time:** 25 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 19–21

## The idea
Good drawing starts with how you sit and hold the pen. Norton says to sit straight, keep the paper in front of your face, and hold the pencil flat and loose. All work is freehand. That means no ruler and no tracing. You will set up Krita the same way. "Hold the pencil almost flat … and as loosely as is consistent with a steady control." (Norton, PDF 19–20)

## Look
PDF 19, Fig. 4: the desk top is tilted so the paper faces the artist. PDF 20, Fig. 5: two hand positions, A for horizontal lines and B for vertical lines.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Press Ctrl + N to make a new document.
2. In the New Document dialog, set Width to 2000 px and Height to 1500 px. Click Create.
3. Click the Freehand Brush Tool in the toolbox.
4. Open the Brush Presets docker. Click a pencil brush preset.
5. In the Tool Options docker, set Smoothing to No Smoothing.
6. Prop up the tablet. Tilt the screen so it nearly faces your face. Sit up straight.
7. Hold the pen almost flat. Hold it loosely. Do not squeeze.
8. Draw one long line across the canvas. Move your whole arm, not only your fingers.
9. Press Ctrl + Z to undo the line. Draw it again.
10. Hold Shift and drag the pen outward. The brush gets bigger. Drag inward to make it smaller.
11. Pick an eraser preset in the Brush Presets docker. Rub out the end of your line.
12. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save the file as ~/Art/Drawing/001-setup.kra.

## Check your work
- Is your screen nearly facing your face?
- Did you draw the line with your whole arm?
- Is the line made in one stroke, not many short ones?

## Make it yours
- Write your name with each of three different pencil presets. Pick your favorite.
- Try the same line with a thick brush and a thin brush.

## New words
- **freehand** — drawn by hand, with no ruler or tracing.
- **brush preset** — a saved brush with its size and settings.
- **undo** — take back your last action with Ctrl + Z.
