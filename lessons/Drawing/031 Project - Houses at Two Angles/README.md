# 031. Project - Houses at Two Angles

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/031-two-angles.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 028 to 030  
**Time:** 60 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 79–80, 130–131, 152–153

## The idea
Each set of parallel lines has its own vanishing point. Two houses at different angles have different points on the same horizon. Norton shows two books turned a little apart. Each book has its own points. Keep the view inside 60 degrees. "when lines cease to be parallel, their vanishing points are different" (Norton, PDF 80)

## Look
PDF 79–80, Figs. 96–99: two books at different angles. PDF 131, Fig. 178: the rule that a picture should not be wider than 60 degrees. PDF 152–153: a street seen from the middle of a crosswalk.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 4000 by 2400 px. Add a horizon guide.
2. Draw a house at the left at about 30 degrees. Use freehand lines and the order from lesson 030.
   You should see: One house with VP1 and VP2 on the horizon.
3. Draw a smaller house at the right at a different angle. It needs two new points on the same horizon.
4. Mark the new points. Draw all of its level lines to them.
5. Draw one road that runs to one of the points.
6. Add doors and windows to each house. Use each house's own points.
7. Add one fence in front. Use the posts method from lesson 024.
8. Pick the Rectangle Tool. Draw a frame that includes only the area of interest.
9. Check: does your frame avoid lines that run into the corners?
10. Fill the roofs, walls, and ground with three values. Use the Fill Tool.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/031-two-angles.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the two houses share one horizon?
- Does each house have its own vanishing points?
- Are all vertical lines vertical?

## Make it yours
- Draw a corner shop and a house next to it.
- Draw three boxes at three angles.

## New words
- **angle** — how much an object is turned from the picture.
- **frame** — the edge you choose for your picture.
- **narrow view** — a view with a small angle.
