# 004. Loose ovals with the whole arm

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/004-ovals.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 003  
**Time:** 25 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 27–29

## The idea
An oval is the shape of a circle seen at a slant. Norton calls it an ellipse. Draw it in one smooth move. First mark the four end points. Then circle in the air above the paper to gain confidence. Then draw the oval in one stroke. "Make the whole outline with one movement, first carrying the pencil evenly several times over the paper without touching it" (Norton, PDF 29)

## Look
PDF 27, Fig. 15 shows an ellipse with its end points marked A and B. PDF 29: where to start a horizontal ellipse and a vertical ellipse.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2000 by 1500 px.
2. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool, a pencil brush preset, and No Smoothing.
3. Make four small dots for an oval. Put two on the long line and two on the short line.
4. Keep the pen above the canvas. Circle over the dots three times in the air.
5. Now draw the oval through the four dots in one stroke. Start left of the top middle. Go right first.
   You should see: An oval that touches all four dots with a smooth curve.
6. Draw five wide horizontal ovals. Make each one rounder than the last.
7. Draw five tall vertical ovals. Start below the middle of the left side. Go up first.
8. Look at each oval. Are both ends equally round? Both sides alike?
9. Press Ctrl + Z on the worst oval. Draw it again.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/004-ovals.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you make each oval in one stroke?
- Do both ends of each oval look the same?
- Did you circle in the air before you drew?

## Make it yours
- Draw ovals inside each other, like rings.
- Draw a bowl as a wide oval with a smaller oval inside.

## New words
- **ellipse** — the shape of a circle seen at a slant.
- **end points** — the two ends of the long line of an ellipse.
- **symmetrical** — the same on both sides of a middle line.
