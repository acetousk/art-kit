# 032. The ellipse

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/032-ellipse.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 004  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 26–28

## The idea
A circle seen at a slant is an ellipse. Hold a can at your eye level. Its top is a straight line. Lower it, and the top opens into an ellipse. The farther the circle is from your eye level, the rounder its ellipse. An ellipse is the same on both sides of its two main lines. "one of the most beautiful, spirited, and subtle of curves" (Norton, PDF 27)

## Look
PDF 26, Figs. 13–14: a cylinder raised to the eye level. PDF 27, Fig. 15: the long and short diameters. PDF 28, Figs. 17–18: ellipses at different heights.

## Steps
1. Hold a can at your eye level. Close one eye. See the top as a line.
2. Lower it a little at a time. Watch the top open into an ellipse.
3. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px. Add a horizon guide.
4. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
5. On the guide, draw a short flat line. This is the top of a can at your eye level.
6. Below the guide, draw four ellipses. Make each one lower and rounder than the one above.
7. Mark the long and short lines inside each ellipse. They must cross in the middle at a right angle.
8. Check: are both halves of each ellipse the same? Look at one end and then the other.
9. Rotate the canvas with the 4 or 6 key. Look at the ellipses from another side. Press 5 to reset.
10. Redraw the worst ellipse.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/032-ellipse.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the top of a can at your eye level a straight line?
- Does the ellipse get rounder as it moves down?
- Are both ends of each ellipse the same?

## Make it yours
- Draw cups at different heights on a shelf.
- Draw ellipses above the eye, like a lamp shade.

## New words
- **long diameter** — the longest line across an ellipse.
- **short diameter** — the shortest line across an ellipse.
- **foreshortening** — a shape looks shorter as it turns toward your eye.
