# 018. Shade with lines

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/018-line-shading.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 013  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 199–202

## The idea
You can show form with lines alone. A set of diagonal lines is a quick way to show shade. Lines close together look dark. Lines far apart look light. A tint of flat lines behind a shape makes it stand out. Where shade is deepest, let the lines run into solid black. "the use of a series of diagonal lines is an effective, and probably the most ready and rapid, method" (Crane, PDF 202)

## Look
PDF 200: a tint of horizontal lines behind a head. PDF 201: apples in deep shade, with lines that run into solid black. PDF 202: diagonal shading.

## Steps
1. Pick a round object, such as an apple or a ball.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2400 by 1800 px.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and the Ink Fineliner preset.
4. Draw the outline of the object.
5. Decide where the light comes from. Say, from the left.
6. Draw diagonal lines on the shade side. Keep them close and parallel.
7. Draw more lines in the darkest part. Run them into solid black at the deepest spot.
   You should see: Shade lines get closer as the form turns away from light.
8. Behind the object, draw a tint of horizontal lines. Keep them open and light.
   You should see: The object stands out from the background.
9. Draw a second object. Use a different line direction for the shade.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/018-line-shading.kra.

## Check your work
- Are the shade lines on the side away from the light?
- Are the lines closer in the darkest part?
- Does the background tint help the object stand out?

## Make it yours
- Shade a cup or a glass jar.
- Draw a face turned to the light, with lines on one side.

## New words
- **shading** — lines or tones that show shadow.
- **diagonal** — slanting from one corner to the opposite corner.
- **tint** — a light tone made of thin lines.
