# 046. The arm from elbow to hand

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/046-arm.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 045  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Constructive Anatomy (Bridgman), PDF pages 70–73, 75, 77

## The idea
The upper arm has one bone. The forearm has two. They turn on each other, so the hand can face up or down. The elbow has three bumps from the back. Draw the arm as a few simple masses, not as muscles. "From the back view, the elbow is seen to have three knobs of bone" (Bridgman, PDF 71)

## Look
PDF 75: the bones of the arm. PDF 77: the forearm turning, palm up and palm down. PDF 73: the names of the arm bones.

## Steps
1. Bend your arm. Feel the elbow bumps.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 2400 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
3. Draw the arm straight. Draw the upper arm as a rounded block. Draw the forearm as a wedge.
4. Add the hand block at the end.
   You should see: A simple arm made of three masses.
5. Draw the same arm bent at the elbow. Keep the length of each part the same.
6. Mark the three bumps at the back of the elbow.
7. Draw the arm with the palm turned up. Show how the forearm twists.
8. Add the cast shadow on one side to show form.
9. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/046-arm.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the forearm the same length in each drawing?
- Did you mark the elbow's bumps?
- Does the twisted forearm look turned, not bent?

## Make it yours
- Draw an arm throwing a ball.
- Draw an arm resting on a table.

## New words
- **humerus** — the bone of the upper arm.
- **elbow** — where the upper arm and forearm join.
- **bone landmark** — a bump on a bone you can see or feel.
