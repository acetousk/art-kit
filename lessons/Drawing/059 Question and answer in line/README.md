# 059. Question and answer in line

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/059-question-answer.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 058  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 51–52, 59–61

## The idea
A line asks for an answer. Draw a curve and you feel the need for a matching curve. Crane calls this question and answer. In a panel, a big mass needs a balancing mass. A diagonal plan puts the second mass near the opposite corner. "one cannot draw a line or define a form without demanding an answer" (Crane, PDF 51)

## Look
PDF 52: a curve and its answer, and five radiating lines with four more added in reverse. PDF 61: two pomegranates balanced across a square by a diagonal line.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3600 by 2000 px. Draw three equal squares with the Rectangle Tool.
2. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and the Ink Fineliner preset.
3. Square 1. Draw one curve, the question. Draw a matching curve below it, the answer.
4. Turn the pair into a scroll. Add a small curl at each end.
5. Square 2. Draw a circle near the top left corner. Use the Ellipse Tool.
6. Draw a balancing circle near the bottom right corner.
7. Join the two with a spiral line. Add small leaves on the line.
   You should see: Two masses balanced across a diagonal.
8. Square 3. Draw a second half that mirrors the first across the center line.
9. Draw five lines that fan out from one point. The mirror draws the other side.
10. Look at all three squares. Where did you feel the pull to answer a line?
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/059-question-answer.kra.

## Check your work
- Does each curve have a matching answer?
- Do the two circles balance each other?
- Does the fan look centered?

## Make it yours
- Design a border for a page.
- Design a small tile with a diagonal plan.

## New words
- **counterbalance** — a mass that balances another mass.
- **motive** — a main shape or idea in a design.
- **radiating** — spreading out from one point.
