# 058. Relief - outline, contrast, and light

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/058-relief.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 019 and 020  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 188–189, 191

## The idea
Crane names three ways to make a form stand out. First, outline alone. Second, contrast with a tone behind it. Third, light and shade with lines that follow the form. Draw one pot three ways and compare them. "if we want to give it force and colour, and to distinguish it from its background more emphatically, we must add to our outline" (Crane, PDF 188)

## Look
PDF 189: the form in outline alone, the form with a half-tone of diagonal lines behind it, and the form with modelling lines and cast shadows.

## Steps
1. Pick a simple pot or vase. Light it from one side.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3600 by 2000 px. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and the Ink Fineliner preset.
3. Draw three equal frames in a row with the Rectangle Tool.
4. In frame 1, draw the pot in outline only.
   You should see: A clear outline with no shading.
5. In frame 2, draw the pot again. Add a half-tone of diagonal lines behind it.
6. Add a dark mass at one side of the pot, such as a cloth.
7. In frame 3, draw the pot again. Add lines that follow its round form on the shade side.
8. Add a cast shadow beside it.
9. Compare the three. Which stands out the most?
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/058-relief.kra.

## Check your work
- Is frame 1 only an outline?
- Does the tint in frame 2 make the pot stand out?
- Do the lines in frame 3 follow the shape of the pot?

## Make it yours
- Do the same three ways with an apple.
- Do the same three ways with your hand.

## New words
- **relief** — how much a form stands out.
- **half-tone** — a middle tone, here made of lines.
- **modelling** — using shade to show roundness.
