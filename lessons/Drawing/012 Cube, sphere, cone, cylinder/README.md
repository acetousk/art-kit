# 012. Cube, sphere, cone, cylinder

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/012-basic-forms.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 004  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 91–92, 94

## The idea
Every object is built from a few simple forms: cube, sphere, cone, cylinder, and pyramid. Crane calls them tests of the eye. A mistake shows right away. Look for these forms in nature and in things you use. A cup is a cylinder. A ball is a sphere. "They certainly afford very close tests of correctness of eye, making any fault in perspective or projection at once apparent." (Crane, PDF 91)

## Look
PDF 92: Crane points to the cone, funnel, sphere, and tube in buds, flowers, and seed pods. PDF 94: cubes in crystals and in the honeycomb.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2400 by 1800 px.
2. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil brush preset.
3. Draw a cube, a sphere, a cone, and a cylinder in a row. Use light lines.
4. Draw each one from memory. If one looks flat, draw it again.
   You should see: Four simple forms, drawn with confident lines.
5. Choose five objects near you, such as a cup, a ball, a box, a lamp, and a pencil.
6. Under your forms, draw each object as a simple form.
7. Next to each, write which form it is: cube, cylinder, or others.
8. Draw a plant, flower, or pod in a simple form. Crane says to look for cones and spheres in them.
9. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/012-basic-forms.kra.

## Check your work
- Can you name the basic form in a cup?
- Do your forms look solid, not flat?
- Did you find a basic form in something from nature?

## Make it yours
- Draw your room's objects as simple forms.
- Draw a house using only boxes and a prism.

## New words
- **form** — a solid shape with width, height, and depth.
- **cone** — a form with a round base and a point.
- **cylinder** — a form with two round ends and straight sides.
