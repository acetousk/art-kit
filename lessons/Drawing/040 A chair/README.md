# 040. A chair

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/040-chair.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 031  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 136–138

## The idea
A chair is more than a box. Its sides slant. Its seat may slope down to the back. Yet it is the same on both halves of its middle line. So its front and back edges go to one point. The sloping lines go to a point straight above or below the usual one. "the chair is symmetrical on its center line" (Norton, PDF 136)

## Look
PDF 136, Figs. 188–190: the chair, its plan, and its front view. PDF 137, Fig. 191: how the seat slopes. PDF 138: the rungs and the curved back.

## Steps
1. Put a chair on the floor. Sit far from it. Look at the chair from a corner.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 3000 px. Add a horizon guide.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Draw the nearest leg as a vertical line.
4. Mark VP1 and VP2 on the guide by drawing the bottom edges as you did for the cube.
5. Draw the floor rectangle between the four feet. Use light lines.
6. Draw the four legs as vertical lines.
7. Draw the seat. Slope it down a little toward the back.
   You should see: The seat slopes, but its front and back edges still go to VP2.
8. Draw the chair back. Its top goes to VP2 as well.
9. Add the side rung and the back bars. Draw them to the same points.
10. Make the chair firm. Keep near lines heavy.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/040-chair.kra.

## Check your work
- Are all four legs vertical?
- Do front and back edges go to one point?
- Does the seat slope slightly to the back?

## Make it yours
- Draw a stool or a rocking chair.
- Draw a table with four legs.

## New words
- **symmetrical** — the same on both sides of the middle.
- **rung** — a bar between the legs of a chair.
- **slope** — a slant up or down.
