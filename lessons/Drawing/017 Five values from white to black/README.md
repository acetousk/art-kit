# 017. Five values from white to black

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/017-five-values.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 016  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Composition (Dow), PDF pages 99–100

## The idea
Now add more tones. Dow says to make a scale of white, black, and three grays. Later you can make a scale of seven. These help your judgment of how far apart tones are. They do not fix your plan. A pencil has little depth, so use ink or paint. "A scale of white, black and three grays (a) will be best for beginning" (Dow, PDF 99)

## Look
PDF 99: the exercise. Pick a textile or a design with many spaces. PDF 100: start with pure landscape, then add figures.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
2. Draw a row of five squares with the Rectangle Tool.
3. Fill the first with white and the last with black.
4. Fill the middle square with a gray halfway between them.
5. Fill the two others with grays halfway between each pair.
   You should see: Five steps. Each one is evenly lighter than the next.
6. Use the Color Sampler Tool on two steps. Check that they differ.
7. Make a landscape with many spaces. Sketch it with the Freehand Brush Tool.
8. Fill each space with one of your five values.
9. Stand back. Squint. Do the darks and lights read well?
10. Change one tone if it does not work.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/017-five-values.kra.

## Check your work
- Are your five steps evenly spaced?
- Can you see all five values in the landscape?
- Does the darkest area draw the eye to a good spot?

## Make it yours
- Make a seven-value scale.
- Use five values on a photo or a real room.

## New words
- **value scale** — a row of tones from light to dark.
- **interval** — the size of the step between two tones.
- **squint** — look through half-closed eyes to see big tones.
