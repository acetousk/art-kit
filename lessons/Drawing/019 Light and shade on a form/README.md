# 019. Light and shade on a form

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/019-light-and-shade.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 018  
**Time:** 40 min  
**Read:** Line and Form (Crane), PDF pages 188–189, 210–211, 232–233

## The idea
Light from one side makes a form stand out. You see a lit plane and a shade plane. A cast shadow falls on the table. Crane says to define the edge where light meets shade. "at once stands out in bold relief in broad planes of light and shade, further emphasized by cast shadows" (Crane, PDF 232–233)

## Look
PDF 189: the same form shown in outline only, then with contrast, then with light and shade. PDF 211: the pen sketch of the man with the hoe, made with light and shade only.

## Steps
1. Place a ball and a box on a table. Put a lamp on one side.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
4. Draw the outline of the ball and the box. Use light lines.
5. Make a new layer. Name it shade.
6. On the ball, find where light meets shade. Draw that edge.
7. Fill the shade side of the ball with a gray tone.
8. Fill the shade sides of the box.
   You should see: The ball and box now look solid.
9. Draw the cast shadow on the table. It points away from the lamp.
10. Fill the cast shadow with a darker gray.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/019-light-and-shade.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the cast shadows point away from the lamp?
- Is there a clear edge between light and shade?
- Does the shade side look darker than the lit side?

## Make it yours
- Move the lamp to the other side. Draw again.
- Shade a cylinder, like a can.

## New words
- **cast shadow** — the shadow an object throws on the surface.
- **relief** — how much a form seems to stand out.
- **light plane** — a flat side of a form facing the light.
