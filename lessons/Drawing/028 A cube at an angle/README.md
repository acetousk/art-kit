# 028. A cube at an angle

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/028-cube-angle.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 023  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 67–69

## The idea
Turn a box so a corner points toward you. Now two sides show. They run to two different points on the eye level. Level lines always vanish on the eye level. Draw the nearest vertical edge first. It is the guide for all other sizes. "Steepness of the horizontal edges, therefore, goes with foreshortened surfaces." (Norton, PDF 69)

## Look
PDF 67, Figs. 76–78: a cube revolved on its front corner. PDF 68, Figs. 80–81: lines to the vanishing points. PDF 69, Figs. 82–83: a correction and a row of cubes.

## Steps
1. Place a box on a table below your eye. Turn it so a corner points at you.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 2200 px. Add a horizon guide high on the canvas.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Draw the nearest vertical edge.
4. From its top, draw the two top edges. They slope up toward the horizon.
   You should see: Two light lines that go left and right from the top corner.
5. Use the Straight Line Tool to extend each line to the horizon. Mark each point. These are VP1 and VP2.
6. From the ends of the two top edges, draw lines to the opposite points. This closes the top face.
7. Draw two more vertical edges at the side corners. Make them shorter than the nearest one.
8. From the foot of the near edge, draw lines to VP1 and VP2. These are the bottom edges.
9. Close the cube. Draw the hidden lines in a lighter tone.
10. Check: do all lines of a pair meet on the horizon?
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/028-cube-angle.kra.

## Check your work
- Do the top edges meet the horizon at two points?
- Are all vertical edges vertical?
- Are the side verticals shorter than the near one?

## Make it yours
- Draw a small box and a big box at the same angle.
- Draw a stack of three boxes.

## New words
- **two-point** — a view with two vanishing points.
- **vertical** — straight up and down, as in nature.
- **eye level** — the height of your eyes, and the horizon in your picture.
