# 036. Cylinders lying down

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/036-cylinders-lying.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 035  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 110–112

## The idea
A cylinder on its side has an axis. The axis runs through the middle from end to end. The end ellipse is always at a right angle to the axis. This stays true when you turn the can. The long diameter moves with the axis. Flowers, wheels, and clocks follow the same rule. "cylindrical objects always appear symmetrical" (Norton, PDF 112)

## Look
PDF 110, Figs. 147–149: a cylinder held at the eye level and below. PDF 111, Figs. 151–152: the long diameter moves. PDF 112, Figs. 153–154: a wheel and a flower.

## Steps
1. Lay a can on its side on a table below your eye. Turn it a little.
2. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px. Add a horizon guide above the can.
3. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset. Draw the axis line, from end to end.
4. At the near end, draw a short line across the axis. It must cross at a right angle.
5. Draw an ellipse along that line. Make its long side match the line.
6. Draw the far end ellipse. Make it rounder and a little smaller.
7. Draw the two side lines. They go toward the horizon.
   You should see: The sides run toward one point on the horizon, like any level lines.
8. Press 4 to rotate the canvas. Turn it until the can stands up. Check that it looks balanced.
9. Press 5 to reset. Fix the worst part.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/036-cylinders-lying.kra.

## Check your work
- Does the ellipse cross the axis at a right angle?
- Does the can look balanced when you rotate the canvas?
- Do both sides go toward the horizon?

## Make it yours
- Draw a wheel from the side and from a slant.
- Draw a rolling pin.

## New words
- **axis** — the line through the middle of a form.
- **symmetry** — the same on both sides.
- **rotate** — turn around a middle point.
