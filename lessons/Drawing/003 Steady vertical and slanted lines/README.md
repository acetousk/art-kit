# 003. Steady vertical and slanted lines

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/003-verticals-slants.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 002  
**Time:** 25 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 20–21

## The idea
Vertical lines go from the top down. Slanted lines are called oblique. Practice all of them again and again. Norton says to draw hard shapes first with a thin light line. If it is wrong, draw over it. Do not erase until the shape is right. "Unfamiliar or difficult exercises should be first carefully sketched with a thin, light line." (Norton, PDF 20)

## Look
PDF 20, Fig. 5, position B: the hand for vertical lines. Look at how the pencil points. It moves from the top downward.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 2000 by 1500 px.
2. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool, a pencil brush preset, and No Smoothing.
3. Draw ten vertical lines. Start at the top of each line. Pull down in one stroke.
4. Draw ten slanted lines that lean to the right. Keep them parallel.
5. Draw ten slanted lines that lean to the left. Keep them parallel.
   You should see: Three groups of parallel lines. Parallel lines never touch.
6. Open the Layers docker. Click the new layer button. Name it sketch.
7. Draw a large square lightly on the sketch layer. Draw over any wrong line. Do not erase.
8. Make another new layer. Name it final.
9. Draw the square again on the final layer. Make each line firm. Use one stroke per side.
10. Hide the sketch layer. Click the eye icon next to it.
   You should see: A clean square on the final layer.
11. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/003-verticals-slants.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you draw each vertical line from top to bottom?
- Are the slanted lines in each group parallel?
- Did you draw over a wrong line instead of erasing it at once?

## Make it yours
- Draw a window with the three line types.
- Draw a picket fence with verticals and slants.

## New words
- **vertical** — straight up and down.
- **oblique** — slanted, not vertical and not horizontal.
- **parallel** — going the same way and never meeting.
