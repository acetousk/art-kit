# 021. Eye level and horizon

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/021-eye-level.kra  
**Builds on:** lesson 009  
**Time:** 35 min  
**Read:** Perspective for Artists (Vicat Cole), PDF pages 20–22, 24, 39; Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF page 58

## The idea
Your eye level is the height of your eyes. In a picture it is a flat line called the horizon. Every level thing above it shows its underside. Every level thing below it shows its top. At your eye level you see only the edge. Decide where the horizon goes before you draw anything else. "The horizon in Nature will be at the exact height of your eye" (Vicat Cole, PDF 22)

## Look
Vicat Cole PDF 22, Figs. 24 and 25: a standing figure has the horizon at its head. A seated figure has the horizon at its waist. PDF 24, Figs. 26 and 27: eye height standing and sitting.

## Steps
1. Take a book. Hold it flat at your eye level. You see only its edge.
2. Lower the book. You see its top. Raise it. You see its bottom.
3. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3000 by 2000 px.
4. Click View ‣ Show Rulers. Click View ‣ Show Guides.
5. Drag from the top ruler down to the middle of the canvas. A guide line appears. This is your horizon.
6. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool and a pencil preset.
7. Draw five stick figures of the same height. Put each head on the guide.
8. Put the feet of far figures near the guide. Put the feet of near figures lower.
   You should see: All heads on one line. Near figures are bigger and their feet are lower.
9. Make a new layer. Move the guide lower, to the waist of a figure. This is a seated viewer.
10. Draw three figures again. Put the guide through the waist of each one.
11. Draw a floor under their feet. Notice how it looks more flat than before.
12. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/021-eye-level.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the horizon at the same height as your eyes?
- With the horizon at the heads, are all heads on one line?
- Can you see the top of a level thing when it is below your eye?

## Make it yours
- Draw a street from a low horizon and from a high horizon.
- Draw a table with the horizon above it, then below it.

## New words
- **eye level** — the height of your eyes.
- **horizon** — the flat line at your eye level.
- **guide** — a line you place on the canvas to help you.
