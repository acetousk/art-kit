# 041. A room at an angle

**You make:** ~/Art/Drawing/041-room-angle.kra  
**Builds on:** lessons 027 and 040  
**Time:** 45 min  
**Read:** Freehand Perspective and Sketching (Norton), PDF pages 128–131

## The idea
Now look into the corner of a room. Two walls are turned away from you. Their top and bottom lines run to two points on the eye level. The wall is shorter at the corner than at the sides. Choose the part you want to show. Do not show more than 60 degrees. "The picture angle should not be over sixty degrees, and is better less." (Norton, PDF 131)

## Look
PDF 128, Fig. 174: a room at an angle, with a door. PDF 129, Figs. 175–176: how the picture plane turns. PDF 130, Fig. 177: a frame that cuts off the corners.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document, 3200 by 2400 px. Add a horizon guide about two thirds up.
2. Draw the corner line, as a vertical. Make it short.
3. From its top and bottom, draw lines to the left and right. They go to two points on the horizon.
4. Draw the side edges. The left and right walls are taller than the corner.
   You should see: A room corner with the ceiling and floor lines running to two points.
5. Draw a window on the left wall. Draw its top and bottom lines to the left point.
6. Draw a door on the right wall. Draw its top to the right point.
7. Add the chair from lesson 040 near the corner.
8. Draw a frame with the Rectangle Tool. Keep lines from running into the frame corners.
9. Fill the walls, floor, and ceiling with three values.
10. Press Ctrl + Shift + S. Save as ~/Art/Drawing/041-room-angle.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the corner wall shorter than the side walls?
- Do the ceiling and floor lines run to two points?
- Is your picture inside about 60 degrees?

## Make it yours
- Draw your own room from a corner.
- Draw a hallway turning at a corner.

## New words
- **picture angle** — how wide a view your picture shows.
- **corner** — where two walls meet.
- **frame** — the edge you choose for your picture.
