# 019. Italic Small Letters on Slanted Guides

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/19-italic-guides.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 018
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 208 and 229; Speedball, PDF page 18 (right half); Speedball, PDF page 20 (right half)

## The idea
When you write a slanted letter, the stems must all lean the same way. Brown says sloping letters need many sloping guide lines. Speedball shows dotted diagonal lines for keeping the slant steady. In Krita, draw straight lines first, then shear them.

> "Dotted diagonal lines illustrate method of maintaining any desired degree of slant" (Speedball, PDF p. 18, right half)

## Look
Brown page 208: see the Spanish script with the slanted guide lines drawn across the page. Speedball page 18, right half: see the dotted diagonal lines under the words.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open lettering-sheet.kra and Save As 19-italic-guides.kra.
2. On Guides, draw a band: y = 80, 240, 400, 520. Same lines as the small letter lessons.
3. Add a paint layer named Slant. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 2 px.
4. Draw 30 vertical lines, 80 px apart, from line 1 to line 4. Hold Shift to keep each straight.
5. Use the slanted guides to keep each stem at the same angle.
   You should see: every vertical line leans the same.
6. Add a paint layer named Italic. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 12 px.
7. Write the word minimum. Make each stem follow a slanted line.
8. Write the word unison and the word wine.
9. Check the letters. Do the round letters lean the same way as the stems?
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/19-italic-guides.kra.

## Check your work
- Do all stems follow the slanted lines?
- Is the lean the same in every letter?
- Are the round letters sloped, too?

## Make it yours
- Write your first name in italic small letters.
- Use a slant of 15 degrees. Is it easier or harder?

## New words
- **slant line** — a guide line that leans to show the slope
- **shear** — to lean a whole shape sideways
- **lean** — the direction a letter tilts
