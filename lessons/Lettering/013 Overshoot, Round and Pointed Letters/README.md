# 013. Overshoot, Round and Pointed Letters

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/13-overshoot.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 012
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 32 and 33; Design With FontForge, PDF page 24 and 25

## The idea
Round letters look shorter than flat letters if they stop at the same line. Brown says the curves of C, G, O and Q should run a little past the top and bottom guide lines. Points of V, W and A should also pass the line. Brown gives a type founder's rule. On a 12 inch letter, O runs over about half an inch and A or V about three quarters of an inch.

> "unless the outsides of their curves run both above and below the guide lines" (Brown, PDF p. 32)

## Look
Brown page 32, middle: find the paragraph on optical illusions. Page 33: see how V and W reach below the line. FontForge page 24: see the H whose bar looks wrong.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 12-narrow-letters.kra and Save As 13-overshoot.kra.
2. Keep guide lines at y = 160 and 480 on Guides. Add a paint layer named Test.
3. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 20 px. Draw H, then O, then H, with all three exactly between the lines.
4. Look at the row from far away. O looks smaller than H.
5. Press Shift+S and turn off Grids so you can place curves freely.
6. Draw a second row. Draw H, then an O that runs 13 px above and below the lines, then H.
   You should see: the new O matches H better.
7. Draw a third row with H, A, H, V.
8. Let the points of A and V run 20 px past the lines.
9. Compare all three rows. Which row looks most even?
10. Press Shift+S and turn Grids on again.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/13-overshoot.kra.

## Check your work
- Does the first O look shorter than the H?
- Does the corrected O look the same height?
- Do the points of A and V pass the base line a little?

## Make it yours
- Test S, C and G for overshoot.
- Make O run 40 px over. Does it look too big?

## New words
- **overshoot** — a curve or point that runs a little past the guide line
- **optical illusion** — something the eye sees that is not what you measure
- **point** — the sharp end of a letter like A or V
