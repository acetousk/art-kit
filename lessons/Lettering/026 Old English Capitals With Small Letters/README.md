# 026. Old English Capitals With Small Letters

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/26-old-english.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 025
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Speedball, PDF page 15 (left half); Brown, PDF pages 156 and 197

## The idea
Speedball's Old English plate shows capitals with swelling curves and fine hairlines. Brown warns that a beginner should be careful when setting Gothic capitals alone to make words. Their outlines do not fit next to each other. Use one capital at the start of a word and small letters after it.

> "the beginner should be extremely wary of attempting to use any Gothic capitals alone to form words" (Brown, PDF p. 156)

## Look
Speedball page 15, left half: see the full Old English capitals and the line Resolutions Testimonials Memorials Engrossed. Notice how only one capital starts each word.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 25-blackletter.kra and Save As 26-old-english.kra.
2. Add a capital band at y = 160 and 480 on Guides.
3. Add a small letter band at y = 640, 800, 960, 1080.
4. Add a vector layer named Caps. Pick the Calligraphy tool. Set Angle 45, Width 40, Fixation 1.
5. Draw the capital R with a thick stem and a hairline curl.
6. Add a second capital T. Make the curves swell and then thin.
7. Add the capital E, M, and S. Keep each one the same height.
8. Write the small letters esolutions below the R. Use your blackletter style.
   You should see: one fancy capital followed by plain narrow letters.
9. Write the word Testimonials in the same way.
10. Compare your R with Speedball's R. Fix one stroke.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/26-old-english.kra.

## Check your work
- Does only the first letter of each word use a fancy capital?
- Do the small letters match the blackletter from last lesson?
- Is the capital the same height as the tall letters?

## Make it yours
- Write your name with an Old English initial.
- Write two words with initials T and M.

## New words
- **initial** — the first letter of a word or paragraph
- **hairline** — the thinnest stroke
- **Old English** — a name for the English form of blackletter
