# 029. Optical Center and Balance

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/29-optical-center.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 028
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Speedball, PDF page 14 (left half); Speedball, PDF page 15 (right half); Speedball, PDF page 17 (right half)

## The idea
Speedball says a panel looks best when balanced on the optical center. The optical center is about five percent above the true center. A mechanical division of space looks dull. Place the lettering so it feels balanced, with the heaviest part a little high. Group the lines in two or three parts and leave clear space between.

> "A harmonious division of the same space, balanced upon optical center, is always more pleasing and effective." (Speedball, PDF p. 14, left half)

## Look
Speedball page 14, left half: see the panel divided into equal parts (dull) and the panel divided into two and three (better). Page 15, right half: see A Bad Layout and A Good Layout.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Press Ctrl+N. Make a 1600 by 2000 px image. Save it as 29-optical-center.kra.
2. Turn on the grid at 40 px. Add a paint layer named Panel.
3. Pick the Rectangle tool with No fill and a brush outline.
4. Draw a panel 1200 by 1600 px, centered.
5. Draw a thin line across the panel at the true center, y = 1000 px.
6. Draw a second line 5 percent of the panel height above it, 80 px higher, at y = 920.
7. This is the optical center.
8. Add a layer named Blocks. Pick the Rectangle tool with Fill set to Foreground color.
9. Draw three gray blocks to stand for three lines of lettering.
10. Make version 1: stack the blocks with equal gaps around the true center.
11. Make version 2: group the blocks as two parts and balance them around the optical center.
   You should see: version 2 feels steadier.
12. Duplicate the layer to compare both. Write which looks better.
13. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/29-optical-center.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the optical center above the true center?
- Did the grouped layout look better than equal gaps?
- Are the lines of lettering balanced around the optical center?

## Make it yours
- Try a three-part layout.
- Move the heavy block to the bottom and see how it feels.

## New words
- **optical center** — the point that looks like the center, a little above the true center
- **balance** — equal weight on both sides of a point
- **group** — a set of lines that belong together
