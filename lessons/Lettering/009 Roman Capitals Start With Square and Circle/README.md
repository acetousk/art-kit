# 009. Roman Capitals Start With Square and Circle

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/09-roman-skeletons.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 007
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 25 and 28; Johnston, PDF pages 273 and 274

## The idea
Brown says the Roman capital is based on the square. Round letters are based on the circle. Johnston calls O the key letter: with an O and an I, you can guess the other letters. The Romans carved these on stone, and they are still the best capitals. In this course, thin-line skeletons come first.

> "Given an O and an I of any alphabet, we can make a very good guess at the forms of the other letters." (Johnston, PDF p. 274)

## Look
Brown page 28: see how each letter sits in a square grid with circles drawn on it. Look at the A, H and O. Johnston page 274: see the three O shapes, circular, narrower and wider.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open lettering-sheet.kra and Save As 09-roman-skeletons.kra.
2. Add a paint layer named Skeleton. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 4 px.
3. With the Rectangle tool, hold Shift and draw four squares of 8 x 8 cells (320 px) in row 1.
4. In the first square, draw O with the Ellipse tool and Shift. It touches all four sides.
5. In the second square, draw H. The stems touch the sides and the bar sits at the middle.
6. In the third square, draw A. The point touches the top and the feet touch the bottom corners.
7. In the fourth square, draw N with two stems and a diagonal.
8. In row 2, draw a tall rectangle 2 cells wide. Draw I in it.
9. Draw a rectangle 4 cells wide and draw E in it.
10. Look at O and I together. Which other letters can you guess from them?
11. Write the guess on the canvas with the Text tool. Name three letters.
12. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/09-roman-skeletons.kra.

## Check your work
- Does O touch all four sides of its square?
- Is the I narrower than H?
- Did you draw the diagonals of A and N from corner to corner?

## Make it yours
- Draw M and W in squares. Are they wider than a square?
- Draw D inside a box. Does it fill the same square as O?

## New words
- **Roman capitals** — the square, carved letters of ancient Rome
- **proportion** — the size of one part compared with another
- **key letter** — one letter that tells you the shape of others
