# 024. Round Gothic Small Letters

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/24-round-gothic.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 005
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 152, 153, 155 and 156; Johnston, PDF page 51

## The idea
Brown says Gothic letters grew from the round Roman uncial. Scribes held the quill upright and wrote with a round swinging movement. The pen angle decides every thick and thin. Italian round Gothic stayed round and clear. Brown says Gothic never had one fixed form, but the spirit of the style is the same everywhere.

> "the broad lines are made on the down right-sloping strokes, and the narrow lines are at right angles to these." (Brown, PDF p. 156)

## Look
Brown page 153: see the Italian Round Gothic small letters a to z. Notice the round bowls and heavy strokes. Johnston page 51: read about Italian writing keeping the round forms.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 05-thick-thin.kra and Save As 24-round-gothic.kra.
2. Delete the old layers. Draw a band on Guides: y = 80, 240, 400, 520.
3. Add a vector layer named Gothic. Pick the Calligraphy tool.
4. Set Width 36, Fixation 1, Angle 45. Use Tablet Pressure off.
5. Draw o as a round shape with a thick left and right side.
6. Draw n and m: thick stems with a round arch.
7. Draw a, b, d, e, g with round bowls and straight stems.
   You should see: thick strokes slope down to the right and thin strokes cross them.
8. Add small feet or serifs at the base of the stems.
9. Write the word moonbeam. Keep the letters close together.
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/24-round-gothic.kra.

## Check your work
- Are the thick strokes the same angle in every letter?
- Are the bowls round, not pointed?
- Is the spacing even in the word?

## Make it yours
- Write your first name in round Gothic.
- Compare round Gothic with your Roman small letters.

## New words
- **Gothic** — a medieval lettering style, round or angular
- **uncial** — a round capital-like hand used before Gothic
- **quill** — a pen made from a bird's feather
