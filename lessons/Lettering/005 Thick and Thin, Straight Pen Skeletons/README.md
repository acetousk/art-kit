# 005. Thick and Thin, Straight Pen Skeletons

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/05-thick-thin.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 004
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Johnston, PDF pages 245 to 248; Brown, PDF page 26

## The idea
Johnston draws letters in plain pencil lines first. These are the "essential forms", the skeleton. A flat pen turns the skeleton into thick and thin strokes. Letters made mostly of diagonals, like A, K, M, V, W and X, look too heavy. A turned pen makes them lighter. The ends of thin strokes need small finishing marks.

> "To give uniformity to the various letters of an alphabet it is necessary to treat similar parts as consistently as possible" (Johnston, PDF p. 248)

## Look
Johnston page 246 and page 247: see the rows of Square Capitals and Round Capitals with their skeleton lines. Page 248: see the oblique letters AKMNVW made with a slanted pen.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 04-pen-angle.kra and Save As 05-thick-thin.kra.
2. Add a vector layer named Straight. Pick the Calligraphy tool. Set Width 36, Fixation 1, Angle 0.
3. Draw the letters I L T H E. Each is 8 cells tall (320 px).
4. Use the grid to stay straight.
   You should see: thick stems and thin bars.
5. Below them, draw V A X with the same Angle 0.
   You should see: both diagonals are heavy. Johnston says these look heavy.
6. Add a vector layer named Slanted. Set Angle to 45. Draw V A X again.
   You should see: one diagonal thin and one thick.
7. Draw M and N at Angle 45. Which strokes are thin now?
8. Switch to Ink-2 Fineliner at 4 px on a paint layer. Draw the pencil skeleton of H, A and O.
9. Place the skeleton next to the pen version of each. Check that the pen letter keeps the skeleton shape.
10. Write one sentence with the Text tool: which letters look heavy with a straight pen?
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/05-thick-thin.kra.

## Check your work
- Do V A X look heavier at Angle 0 than at Angle 45?
- Does a straight pen give thin horizontal strokes?
- Did you keep each letter's skeleton the same in both versions?

## Make it yours
- Draw the letters K and W both ways.
- Add a tiny serif to the end of one thin stroke.

## New words
- **skeleton** — the simplest line version of a letter
- **essential form** — the part of a letter you cannot remove
- **oblique** — slanting, not straight up or across
