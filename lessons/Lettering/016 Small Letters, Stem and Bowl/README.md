# 016. Small Letters, Stem and Bowl

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/16-stem-and-bowl.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 015
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 80, 86 and 87; Design With FontForge, PDF pages 79 and 80

## The idea
Most small letters are stems and bowls. FontForge says to start with o and n. The o sets the round shape and the white inside it. The n is a stem and an arch. Brown also gives spacing: space between straight stems is about one and a half squares. Space between round letters is about one square. Round letters sit nearer together.

> "The round letters ... should be spaced nearer together" (Brown, PDF p. 80)

## Look
Brown page 86 and page 87: see the Venetian alphabet. Find the o, n, b, d, p and q. FontForge page 80: see the n broken into a stem and a curve.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 15-small-letter-zones.kra and Save As 16-stem-and-bowl.kra.
2. Hide the old Zones layer. Add a paint layer named Bowls. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 14 px.
3. Pick the Ellipse tool.
4. Draw o on the grid, 4 cells wide and 4 cells tall, between the x-height and base lines.
5. Draw n: a stem, then an arch that leaves the stem, then a second stem.
6. Draw b: a stem from the ascender line, then an o touching it. Draw d the same, mirrored.
7. Draw p and q the same way, with the stem to the descender line.
8. Draw h, m and u from the n shape. Reuse the same arch each time.
   You should see: the stems and arches look like one family.
9. Write n n n n n with a gap of about 80 px between stems.
10. Write o o o o o with a gap of about 53 px.
11. Write the word noon. Do the gaps look even?
12. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/16-stem-and-bowl.kra.

## Check your work
- Do b, d, p and q all use the same o?
- Are the gaps between the o letters smaller than between the n letters?
- Do h, m and u use the same arch as n?

## Make it yours
- Write the word bumpy.
- Make a second o with thicker strokes. How does it change the spacing?

## New words
- **bowl** — the round part of a letter, such as in b or p
- **counter** — the white space inside a letter
- **arch** — the curved top of n, m or h
