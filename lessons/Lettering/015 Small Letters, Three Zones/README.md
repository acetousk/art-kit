# 015. Small Letters, Three Zones

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/15-small-letter-zones.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 008
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 80, 81 and 84; Speedball, PDF page 9 (right half)

## The idea
Brown says the body of a small letter fills a three-unit square. The ascenders rise above it and the descenders fall below. He says to keep the descenders shorter than the ascenders, about two fifths to three fifths. In this course the body is 4 cells, the ascender 4 cells, and the descender 3 cells. That is about two thirds.

> "In general a better result is obtained by making both ascenders and descenders of less than the length of the body" (Brown, PDF p. 81)

## Look
Brown page 84: see the Italian small letters by Cresci. Find the tall letters b, d, h, k, l and the tail letters g, p, q, y. Page 80: read about the diagram of small letters.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open lettering-sheet.kra and Save As 15-small-letter-zones.kra.
2. On Guides, draw band 1: y = 80 (ascender), 240 (x-height), 400 (base), 520 (descender).
3. Draw band 2: y = 680, 840, 1000, 1120.
4. Add a paint layer named Zones. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 10 px.
5. Band 1: draw n n n n n n between the x-height line and the base line.
6. Then draw h b d k l. Each stem runs from the base line to the ascender line.
7. Band 2: draw p q g y j. Each tail runs from the x-height line to the descender line.
8. Add a row with the word ground. Check which letters use all three zones.
9. Check the sizes: ascender part = 4 cells, descender part = 3 cells.
   You should see: tails a little shorter than the tall stems.
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/15-small-letter-zones.kra.

## Check your work
- Are the ascenders longer than the descenders?
- Do the bodies of all letters stop at the x-height line?
- Did every tail reach the descender line?

## Make it yours
- Write the word handbag.
- Make descenders the same length as ascenders. Does it look better or worse?

## New words
- **ascender** — a part that rises above the x-height
- **descender** — a part that falls below the base line
- **x-height** — the height of the body of small letters
