# 030. Margins and Fitting Lines

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/30-margins-fitting.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 029
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Johnston, PDF pages 260, 264 and 269; Brown, PDF pages 229 and 230

## The idea
Johnston says a margin looks larger when the text is heavier. If you spread out the letters, make the margins wider. He gives a margin ratio for books, 1.5 : 2 : 3 : 4. If a line is too short or too long, Brown says to widen or narrow letters and change spaces. Johnston offers other ways to fit lines: smaller end letters, linking, and tying up.

> "A given margin looks larger the heavier the mass of the text." (Johnston, PDF p. 269)

## Look
Johnston page 269: see the figure with three versions of the same lines. Page 264: read about ending with smaller letters. Brown page 229: read how to make two lines equal in length.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Press Ctrl+N. Make a 2400 by 1600 px image. Save it as 30-margins-fitting.kra.
2. Add a Panel layer. Draw a rectangle outline, 2000 by 1200 px, centered.
3. Inside the panel, mark margins with a faint line: top 160, sides 200, bottom 280 px.
4. Add a paint layer named Text. Use Ink-2 Fineliner at 10 px.
5. Write two lines in Roman capitals: THE ART OF / LETTERING. Make both fit between the side margins.
6. Make line 2 fit exactly. Widen the letters of the short line and narrow the long line a little.
   You should see: both lines the same length.
7. Add a short third line. End it with one smaller word. Compare it with the other lines.
8. Check the margins. Are they wider at the bottom than at the top?
9. Make a second version with letters spaced wider. Make the margins wider, too.
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/30-margins-fitting.kra.

## Check your work
- Are the margins larger at the bottom than the top?
- Do both lines have the same length?
- Did wider letter spacing need wider margins?

## Make it yours
- Fit three lines of different length into one block.
- Try a block of text with a ragged right edge.

## New words
- **margin** — the empty border around the lettering
- **ragged** — with an uneven edge
- **tie up** — to add a word above or below the line with a stroke
