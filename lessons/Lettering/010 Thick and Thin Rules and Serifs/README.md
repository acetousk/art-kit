# 010. Thick and Thin Rules and Serifs

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/10-thick-thin-serifs.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 009
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 26 and 27; Johnston, PDF page 248

## The idea
Brown gives three rules for thick and thin lines. First, never thicken horizontal lines. Second, thicken the sloping lines that go down from left to right. Third, thicken the upright lines, except in N. A serif is a small cross-stroke that finishes the free end of a line. Brown says serifs came from chisel marks and pen letters.

> "The "serif" — a cross-stroke or tick — finishes the free ends of all lines used in making a Roman capital." (Brown, PDF p. 27)

## Look
Brown page 26: find the three rules in the text. Look at A and N on page 28 and see which lines are thick. Johnston page 248: see the four kinds of finishing stroke.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 09-roman-skeletons.kra and Save As 10-thick-thin-serifs.kra.
2. Hide the squares. Add a paint layer named Letters. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner.
3. Use size 20 px for thick, 6 px for thin.
4. Draw I with one thick stem (20 px).
5. Draw H: two thick stems, one thin bar (6 px).
6. Draw T and E: thick stem, thin bars. Remember: never thicken a horizontal line.
7. Draw A: thin left line, thick right line. Draw V: thick left line, thin right line.
   You should see: the down-right strokes are thick.
8. Draw N: thin stems, thick diagonal. This is the exception in rule three.
9. Add a serif at each free end. Use 6 px short cross-strokes about 1 cell wide.
10. Compare the serif on a thick stem with the serif on a thin stroke.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/10-thick-thin-serifs.kra.

## Check your work
- Are all horizontal bars thin?
- Is the right line of A thicker than the left?
- Does every free end have a serif?

## Make it yours
- Try Z: which line should be thick?
- Draw the same letters with no serifs. Which looks cleaner?

## New words
- **serif** — a small cross-stroke at the end of a letter's stroke
- **thick stroke** — the heavy part of a letter
- **hairline** — the thinnest line in a letter
