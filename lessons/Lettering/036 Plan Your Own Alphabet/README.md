# 036. Plan Your Own Alphabet

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/36-type-plan.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 034
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Design With FontForge, PDF pages 5, 6, 8, 10, 33 and 34

## The idea
A type designer plans before drawing. FontForge says construction is the skeleton and the rest is the flesh: width, weight, terminals. Choose a few traits. Keep the first project small. FontForge says to improve a small core set of letters before adding more. In this course you will draw a rounded monoline alphabet on a grid.

> "Construction is the skeleton of the glyph, while the rest — width, weight, terminals — are all parts of the flesh." (Design With FontForge, PDF p. 5)

## Look
FontForge page 6: see the large and small x-height and the ascender height. Page 8: see wide and narrow letters. Page 10: see letters with contrast and without it. Page 34: read the advice to include less.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Press Ctrl+N. Make a 2000 by 1400 px image. Save it as 36-type-plan.kra.
2. Use the Text tool. Write a title: My Alphabet Plan.
3. Write your feeling words for the alphabet, for example friendly, round, clear.
4. Write your choices: stroke weight 50 px, no contrast, no serifs, round ends on strokes.
5. Write the proportions: x-height 250 px, cap height 350 px, em square 500 px.
6. Choose a name for your typeface. Write it at the top.
7. Pick the test word adhesion. FontForge says these letters give a good base.
8. Sketch adhesion three times on a Sketch layer. Use Ink-2 Fineliner at 12 px. Make each version different in width.
9. Circle the version that fits your feeling words.
10. Write one line: what is the one trait that will make my font different?
   You should see: a one-page plan with one circled sketch.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/36-type-plan.kra.

## Check your work
- Does your plan name a feeling?
- Did you choose the weight and the proportions?
- Is the plan small enough to finish?

## Make it yours
- Plan a second font with serifs.
- Plan a font with contrast between thick and thin.

## New words
- **typeface** — a complete design of letters, numbers and signs
- **glyph** — one letter, number or sign in a font
- **trait** — one choice that gives a font its look
