# 028. Word Space and Line Space

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/28-word-line-space.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 027
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Johnston, PDF pages 82, 83 and 260; Design With FontForge, PDF pages 88 and 89

## The idea
Johnston says words are kept as close as possible while staying easy to read. The usual space between two words is the width of the letter o. Lines of small letters are about three times the height of o apart, so that the ascenders and descenders just clear. FontForge says a word space that is too narrow can ruin a design.

> "The average space between two words is the width of the letter o" (Johnston, PDF p. 82)

## Look
Johnston page 83: see the lines marked distance apart of writing-lines. Page 260: find Words, commonly one letter-space apart. FontForge page 88: see the word space that is too tight, too wide and just right.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 17-small-alphabet.kra and Save As 28-word-line-space.kra.
2. Hide the old layers. Pick the Guides layer.
3. Draw band 1 at y = 80, 240, 400, 520. Draw band 2 at y = 560, 720, 880, 1000.
4. Add a paint layer named Words. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 12 px.
5. Write line 1: the quick brown fox. Make each word space as wide as your o (about 4 cells).
   You should see: words that are easy to tell apart.
6. Write line 2: jumps over the lazy dog. Check that the descender of p touches nothing.
7. Check the line space. Baseline to baseline is 480 px, three times the height of o.
8. Make a copy of line 1 with word spaces half as wide. Compare.
9. Make another copy with word spaces twice as wide. Compare.
10. Which word space reads best? Write your answer on the canvas with the Text tool.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/28-word-line-space.kra.

## Check your work
- Is your word space about as wide as an o?
- Do descenders clear the ascenders on the next line?
- Does half-width word space make the words run together?

## Make it yours
- Write a rhyme in four lines.
- Use a larger line space. What changes?

## New words
- **word space** — the gap between two words
- **line space** — the distance from one line of writing to the next
- **clear** — to pass without touching
