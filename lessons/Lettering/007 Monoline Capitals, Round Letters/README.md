# 007. Monoline Capitals, Round Letters

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/07-monoline-round-caps.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 006
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Speedball, PDF page 7 (right half); Speedball, PDF page 24 (left half)

## The idea
Round letters are built from circles and parts of circles. Speedball says to overlap strokes where circles join. O is the key round letter. C, G, D, Q, S, J, U, B, P and R all use parts of it. A round letter should look as tall as a straight one. You will fix that in a later lesson.

> "When joining circular elements OVERLAP strokes." (Speedball, PDF p. 7, right half)

## Look
Page 7, right half, top: the strokes numbered 5 to 9 show how circles and half circles join. Page 24, left half: see how round the O, C, G, Q are in Plain Gothic.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 06-monoline-straight-caps.kra and Save As 07-monoline-round-caps.kra.
2. Hide the Caps layer. Add a new paint layer named Round. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 12 px.
3. Pick the Ellipse tool. Hold Shift and drag a circle in row 1, 8 cells wide. This is O.
4. Draw C: a circle with an opening on the right. Erase the gap with the Eraser (E).
5. Draw G like C with a short bar on the right. Draw Q like O with a tail.
6. Draw D: a stem, then a half circle. Let the stroke overlap the stem.
7. In row 2, draw U, J, B, P and R. Use half circles and stems. Overlap each join.
   You should see: no gaps and no lumps at the joins.
8. In row 3, draw S. Use two small circles, one above the other, and connect them.
9. Compare O with the H from the last lesson. Does O look as tall?
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/07-monoline-round-caps.kra.

## Check your work
- Are your round letters all about the same width?
- Do the joins overlap with no gap?
- Does S look balanced, not leaning?

## Make it yours
- Write the word GOOD. Check that all the O letters match.
- Draw a circle O with a ruler-straight stem and no overlap. Compare the join.

## New words
- **circular element** — a curved part of a letter built from a circle
- **join** — the place where two strokes meet
- **key letter** — a letter that guides the shape of others
