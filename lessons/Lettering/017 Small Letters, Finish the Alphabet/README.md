# 017. Small Letters, Finish the Alphabet

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/17-small-alphabet.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 016
**Time:** 45 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 84, 86 and 87; Design With FontForge, PDF pages 108 and 111

## The idea
Once you have o, n and the stems, build the rest from parts. FontForge says i comes from the stem of n, and d comes from the stem of h plus o. The letters a, e, g, s, t and f need more care. The letters v, w, x, y and z are diagonals. Brown's Venetian alphabets show good forms to copy.

> "The 'i' can be derived from the stem of the 'n'." (Design With FontForge, PDF p. 108)

## Look
Brown page 86 and 87: see the big small letters a to z and the Venetian alphabet. Check the a, e, g, s, f and t. FontForge page 108: see i, l, d and h built from parts.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 16-stem-and-bowl.kra and Save As 17-small-alphabet.kra.
2. Hide the old layer. Add a paint layer named Alphabet. Keep Ink-2 Fineliner at 14 px.
3. Band 1: draw a b c d e f g h i j k l m.
4. Copy o for a, c, e. Copy the stem for the tall letters.
5. Draw the dots of i and j round, right above the stem.
6. Band 2: draw n o p q r s t u v w x y z.
   You should see: 26 letters with the same stroke width.
7. Draw u by turning your n upside down. Use the Transform tool and rotate 90 degrees twice.
8. Draw v and w with straight diagonals, w as two v shapes.
9. Draw x and k with straight strokes. Draw z with a top and base bar and a diagonal.
10. Check each letter's height. Do any look too small or too large?
11. Write the word quickly. Check the letter shapes together.
12. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/17-small-alphabet.kra.

## Check your work
- Do a, c and e share the shape of o?
- Is u a turned n?
- Do all letters have the same stroke width?

## Make it yours
- Write the pangram: the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.
- Redraw your two least favorite letters.

## New words
- **alphabet** — all the letters of a writing system in order
- **pangram** — a sentence that uses every letter
- **family likeness** — parts of letters that match each other
