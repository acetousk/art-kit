# 020. Italic Capitals and Swashes

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/20-italic-capitals.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 019
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 215, 219 and 221; Johnston, PDF page 319

## The idea
Italic capitals are Roman capitals slightly sloped. Johnston says they are often sloped less than the small letters. They may be flourished freely. Printers of the 1600s called these swash letters. A swash is a long free tail or curl on a letter. Use swashes on Q, R, K and A. Keep the rest of the letter simple.

> "Italic Capitals are a variety of the Roman Capitals, slightly sloped" (Johnston, PDF p. 319)

## Look
Brown page 221: see the Modern American Italic Capitals. Page 215: see the Spanish Script Capitals A to Z. Page 219: see how Bruce Rogers adds swashes.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 19-italic-guides.kra and Save As 20-italic-capitals.kra.
2. Hide the Italic layer. Draw guide lines for a capital band at y = 160 and 480 on Guides.
3. Add a paint layer named Caps. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 14 px.
4. Draw the capitals A B C D E F with a slant of about 6 degrees.
5. Lean them less than your small letters.
6. Draw G H I J K L. Give the J a long curved tail.
7. Draw Q with a long swash tail under the next letter. Draw R with a long swash leg.
8. Draw M N O P S T. Keep the curves smooth.
9. Add one swash to A. Draw it from the crossbar as a loop.
   You should see: swashes that curve smoothly and do not touch other letters.
10. Write your first name. Use a swash on the first letter.
11. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/20-italic-capitals.kra.

## Check your work
- Are the capitals sloped less than the small letters?
- Does the swash tail curve smoothly?
- Is every capital the same height?

## Make it yours
- Write the name of a friend with a big swash capital.
- Try three different Q tails.

## New words
- **swash** — a long free tail or curl on a letter
- **flourish** — a decorative curve added to a letter
- **capital** — a large letter used for names and starts
