# 018. Italic, Narrow and Slanted

**You make:** ~/Art/Lettering/18-italic-slope.kra
**Builds on:** lesson 017
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** Brown, PDF pages 206 and 207; Johnston, PDF pages 315 and 319; Design With FontForge, PDF page 136

## The idea
Brown says italic is, in theory, the Roman letter sloped. In practice you must change some forms after you slope them. Johnston says italics are slightly narrower and slightly sloped. They are the most rapid of formal hands. FontForge says most modern italics slant between 6 and 9 degrees. A slanted Roman is not a true italic, but it is a good first step.

> "Italics closely resemble the Roman Small-Letters, but are slightly narrowed, slightly sloped" (Johnston, PDF p. 315)

## Look
Brown page 206: find the first paragraph on italic. Johnston page 319: see the capitals sloped less than the small letters. FontForge page 136: see the slant range.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open 17-small-alphabet.kra and Save As 18-italic-slope.kra.
2. Select the Alphabet layer. Duplicate it with the Layers menu, Duplicate Layer or Mask.
3. Draw each letter with stems that lean at the same angle.
   You should see: the letters lean to the right.
4. Select the sheared layer. Pick the Transform tool. Scale the width to 90 percent.
5. Compare a sloped n with your first n. Do the stems keep the same spacing?
6. Add a paint layer named Italic. Pick Ink-2 Fineliner at 14 px.
7. Draw a new n by hand: start the arch from the middle of the stem, not from the top.
8. Draw o as a narrower oval. Draw a, d and g with the same lean.
9. Compare your hand drawn letters with the sloped ones. Which look more natural?
10. Press Ctrl+S to save to ~/Art/Lettering/18-italic-slope.kra.

## Check your work
- Is the italic narrower than the Roman?
- Does the slant look about 8 degrees?
- Did your hand drawn n start the arch lower than the stem top?

## Make it yours
- Try a slant of 4 degrees and a slant of 14 degrees.
- Try an upright italic with no slant at all.

## New words
- **italic** — a narrow, slanted style of small letters
- **slant** — how far a letter leans from straight up
- **faux italic** — a Roman letter only sloped, without changes
