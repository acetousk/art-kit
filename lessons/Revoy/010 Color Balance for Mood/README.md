# 010. Color Balance for Mood

**You make:** ~/Art/Revoy/010-color-balance.kra  (new file, copied from lesson 009)
**Builds on:** lesson 009
**Time:** 30 min
**Read:** David Revoy, "Overview of my Krita Digital Painting Workflow − end 2022", section Post; Krita Manual, Color Balance

## The idea
At the end, Revoy changes the colors of the whole picture. He uses Color Balance to shift shadows and highlights. He says: "I also use very often the Color Balance this way" (David Revoy, Overview of my Krita Digital Painting Workflow). Small changes often work best.

## Look
In the Krita Manual, read the Color Balance section. It has sliders for shadows.

## Steps
1. Open ~/Art/Revoy/009-light-glow.kra. Do not save over it.
2. Press File ‣ Save As. Use ~/Art/Revoy/010-color-balance.kra.
3. Choose Filters ‣ Adjust ‣ Color Balance.
4. Move the shadow sliders a little toward blue.
5. Move the highlight sliders a little toward warm yellow.
   You should see: The shadows look cooler. The light looks warmer.
6. Hide the Color Balance layer, then show it again.
7. Keep the change small. Undo with Ctrl+Z if it is too strong.
8. Save the file.

## Check your work
- Does Color Balance change the colors of the whole picture? (Yes.)
- Must the change be big? (No.)
- Does Revoy use Color Balance near the end? (Yes.)

## Make it yours
- Turn one picture into a warm sunset and a cool night.
- Make a small change and a big change. Compare them.

## New words
- **Color Balance** — a filter that shifts color in shadows, midtones, and highlights
- **shadow** — the darkest part of a shape
- **warm** — colors with yellow or red
