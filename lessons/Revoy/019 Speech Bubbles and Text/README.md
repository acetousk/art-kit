# 019. Speech Bubbles and Text

**You make:** ~/Art/Revoy/019-speech-bubbles.kra  (new file, copied from lesson 018)
**Builds on:** lesson 018
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** David Revoy, "Tutorial: a Comic strip from A to Z with Krita", chapter Speechbubbles; Krita Manual, Text Tool

## The idea
Speech bubbles carry the words of a comic. Revoy spends time on them. He says he spends time on "the creation of speechbubbles, text" (David Revoy, Tutorial: a Comic strip from A to Z with Krita). Keep the text large and clear. Put the bubble near the speaker.

## Look
In the comic tutorial, read the chapter Speechbubbles. In the Krita Manual, read the Text Tool page.

## Steps
1. Open ~/Art/Revoy/018-light-texture.kra. Do not save over it.
2. Press File ‣ Save As. Use ~/Art/Revoy/019-speech-bubbles.kra.
3. Add a new paint layer above all. Name it bubbles.
4. Pick the Elliptical Selection Tool. Draw an oval in the top panel.
5. Pick the Fill Tool. Fill the oval with white.
6. Pick the Polygon Tool. Draw a small tail to the speaker. Fill it with white.
7. Pick the Text Tool. Click inside the bubble. Type Hi!
   You should see: The word sits inside the white bubble.
8. Make the text black and large enough to read.
9. Save the file.

## Check your work
- Is a speech bubble the place for words? (Yes.)
- Must the words be small? (No.)
- Is the bubble near its speaker? (Yes, it should be.)

## Make it yours
- Write two lines in one bubble.
- Make a thought bubble with three small circles.

## New words
- **speech bubble** — a shape that holds spoken words
- **tail** — the small point that shows who speaks
- **Text Tool** — the Krita tool for typing words
