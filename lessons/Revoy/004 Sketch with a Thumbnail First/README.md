# 004. Sketch with a Thumbnail First

**You make:** ~/Art/Revoy/004-thumbnail-sketch.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 003
**Time:** 35 min
**Read:** David Revoy, "Overview of my Krita Digital Painting Workflow − end 2022", sections Storyboard/thumbnail and Sketch

## The idea
Revoy starts with a rough picture. It answers what to paint and where to paint it. He says: "Preparing a thumbnail, or a sketch with a large brush always helps me" (David Revoy, Overview of my Krita Digital Painting Workflow). Then he draws thin lines on a layer with less opacity. The lines are a guide, not final art.

## Look
In the article, compare the Storyboard/thumbnail part with the Sketch part. Note how the sketch lines look thin.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Press Ctrl+N. Make a new file, 2400 by 1600 pixels.
2. Double-click the first layer name. Rename it thumbnail.
3. Pick b3) Basic Oval Brush - deevad 25. Use a large brush size.
4. Draw three big shapes: a character, a tree, and the ground.
   You should see: Big, simple shapes that show the scene.
5. Add a new paint layer above it. Name it sketch.
6. Pick c1) Pencil H Sketch - deevad 25. Draw thin lines over the shapes.
7. Lower the sketch layer opacity to about 30 percent in the Layers docker.
8. Hide the thumbnail layer. Look at the sketch alone.
   You should see: Thin lines show the character and the tree.
9. Save the file with File ‣ Save As. Use ~/Art/Revoy/004-thumbnail-sketch.kra.

## Check your work
- Does the thumbnail solve the scene before the sketch? (Yes.)
- Must the sketch lines be clean? (No.)
- Is the sketch on its own layer? (Yes.)

## Make it yours
- Make three thumbnails of one subject. Pick the best one.
- Sketch a cat with thin lines. Keep the shapes simple.

## New words
- **thumbnail** — a small, rough first version of a picture
- **opacity** — how see-through a layer is
- **sketch layer** — a layer with thin guide lines
