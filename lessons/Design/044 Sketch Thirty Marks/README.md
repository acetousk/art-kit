# 044. Sketch Thirty Marks

**You make:** ~/Art/Design/044-thumbnails.kra (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 043
**Time:** 45 min
**Read:** Design Fundamentals (Hass 2015), PDF pages 46–47; A Book of American Trade-Marks (Sinel 1924), PDF page 6; Krita Manual, Freehand Brush Tool; Krita Manual, Rectangle Tool

## The idea
Quantity matters in early sketches. Hass says to get as many ideas down as you can, and not to judge them yet. First, brainstorm many different ideas. Then take one good idea and draw many variations of it. Sinel adds that a picture or shape can speak to everyone.

> "Quantity is very important in thumbnails!" (Design Fundamentals, PDF page 47)

## Look
Hass page 47: read the two approaches to thumbnails, and the word roughs. Sinel page 6: read the paragraph on objects and abstract forms.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document 1800 x 1500 px at 150 ppi.
2. Choose a made-up business: a bike shop called "Spoke". Or pick your own.
3. Pick the Rectangle Tool with Fill set to Not filled.
4. Draw a grid of 6 columns by 5 rows. That makes 30 small boxes.
5. Pick the Freehand Brush Tool. Choose a pencil brush preset. Set a size that fits inside the boxes.
6. Fill 15 boxes with different ideas. Use objects, letters, and shapes. Spend about one minute on each.
7. Do not judge yet. Draw fast.
   You should see many different ideas.
8. Pick your best idea so far.
9. Fill the other 15 boxes with variations of that one idea. Change the shape, the angle, and the parts.
   You should see one idea in many forms.
10. Mark your best three with a star using the Text Tool.
11. Cross out nothing. Keep all.
12. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/044-thumbnails.kra.

## Check your work
- Did you fill all 30 boxes?
- Do your first 15 differ from each other?
- Do your last 15 explore one idea?

## Make it yours
- Do 30 thumbnails for your own initials.
- Do 30 for a club name in your school.

## New words
- **brainstorm** — to list many ideas without judging them
- **variation** — a small change made on one idea
- **rough** — a bigger sketch of a chosen idea
