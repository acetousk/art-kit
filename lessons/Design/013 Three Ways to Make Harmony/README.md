# 013. Three Ways to Make Harmony

**You make:** ~/Art/Design/013-three-ways.kra (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 012
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Composition (Dow 1913), PDF pages 31–33; Krita Manual, Straight Line Tool; Krita Manual, Bezier Curve Tool

## The idea
Arthur Dow taught that good design is good spacing. He named five ways to make harmony. Here you try three. Opposition is two lines meeting. Transition softens that meeting with a third line or a curve. Subordination puts one main part first and the rest under it, like a tree trunk and branches.

> "Two straight lines meeting in opposing directions give an impression of abruptness, severity, or even violence" (Composition, PDF page 32)

## Look
Dow page 31: find the numbered list of five ways. Page 32: see the three small sketches under Transition. Page 33: read the three ways to build Subordination.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document 1500 x 2000 px at 150 ppi.
2. Pick the Straight Line Tool. Draw two thick lines that meet at a sharp corner. This is Opposition.
3. Look at it. It feels sharp.
4. Below it, draw the same two lines. Add a third line across the corner.
   You should see the sharp meeting feel softer.
5. Pick the Bezier Curve Tool. Draw the same corner, but change the sharp corner into a curve. This is Transition.
6. In the lower third, draw one tall main line. This is the trunk.
7. Draw four branches from it, each shorter than the trunk.
8. Draw two tiny twigs on each branch. This is Subordination.
   You should see one clear leader and smaller followers.
9. Use the Text Tool to write each name in small type under its drawing.
10. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/013-three-ways.kra.

## Check your work
- Does your first corner feel sharp?
- Did the third line or the curve soften the corner?
- Does your tree have one main line that leads?

## Make it yours
- Draw a skyline where one tower leads and others follow.
- Draw a bracket for a shelf by changing straight lines to a curve.

## New words
- **opposition** — two lines meeting in different directions
- **transition** — a soft link between two directions
- **subordination** — one main part with smaller parts under it
