# 015. Notan Landscape in Two and Three Values

**You make:** ~/Art/Design/015-notan-landscape.kra (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 014
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** Composition (Dow 1913), PDF pages 79,92–93; Krita Manual, Polygon Tool; Krita Manual, Fill Tool; Krita Manual, Layers

## The idea
A landscape is a good subject for notan. Its shapes are uneven, so they are fun to arrange. First, design it as flat black and white. Then add one middle gray. Dow says a middle tone opens a new field for design. Think in shapes, not in shadows.

> "The next step is to mix three values" (Composition, PDF page 93)

## Look
Dow page 79: read how landscape is first treated as a design. Page 92 and 93: read the three value study and the "Three Grays, A Scale" paragraph.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document 1500 x 2000 px at 150 ppi.
2. Draw a rough landscape with the Straight Line Tool. Add a horizon, a hill, a tree, and a moon.
3. Add a Paint Layer under the lines. Name it "two values".
4. Fill the sky white. Fill the ground black with the Fill Tool.
5. Fill the tree black or white so it stands out from what is behind it.
   You should see strong shapes with no gray.
6. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/015-notan-landscape.kra.
7. Duplicate the layer. Name it "three values". Rename the first to keep it.
8. Pick a middle gray. Use the Fill Tool to fill the hill with gray.
9. Fill the sun or moon with the lightest tone and the nearest tree with black.
   You should see a feeling of distance.
10. Draw a small scale of three boxes: white, gray, black, next to the picture.
11. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/015-notan-landscape.kra.

## Check your work
- Does each shape have one flat value?
- Is the gray clearly in the middle, not close to black or white?
- Which picture feels deeper, the two or the three value one?

## Make it yours
- Make a night city skyline in three values.
- Do a landscape of your street in two values.

## New words
- **horizon** — the line where land meets sky
- **middle value** — a gray halfway between black and white
- **scale** — a set of steps from light to dark
