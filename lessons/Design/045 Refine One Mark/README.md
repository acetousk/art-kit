# 045. Refine One Mark

**You make:** ~/Art/Design/045-mark-rough.kra (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 044
**Time:** 45 min
**Read:** A Book of American Trade-Marks (Sinel 1924), PDF pages 6,29,36; Design Fundamentals (Hass 2015), PDF page 47; Krita Manual, Freehand Brush Tool; Krita Manual, Layers

## The idea
Now choose and refine. Pick your three best thumbnails and make a bigger rough of each. Test each one small, in one color, and with the name. Sinel says an irregular shape is more distinctive than a plain frame. He thought the Fisher Body mark would be better without its rectangle. Test that on your own mark.

> "registers a much quicker and more lasting impression" (A Book of American Trade-Marks, PDF page 6)

## Look
Sinel page 6: read about the Fisher Body mark. Page 29: see the Fisher mark. Page 36: see the dog mark, a good shape without a frame. Hass page 47: read about roughs.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Open ~/Art/Design/044-thumbnails.kra.
2. Pick the Rectangular Selection Tool. Select one of your three starred thumbnails. Press Ctrl + C.
3. Press Ctrl + N. Set Width 1800 px, Height 1200 px, Resolution 150 ppi. Click Create.
4. Press Ctrl + V. Use the Transform Tool to make the thumbnail bigger. Move it to the left.
5. Repeat for the other two thumbnails. Place all three side by side.
6. Pick a pencil brush preset. Redraw each mark big and clean on a new Paint Layer.
7. Make each mark flat black shapes. Remove any shading.
8. Add a Paint Layer. Make a small copy of each mark 100 px wide.
   You should see which marks still read when tiny.
9. Make a white copy of each mark on a black box.
10. Write your business name next to each mark with the Text Tool.
11. Delete any frame you do not need on one version. Compare it with the framed one.
12. Mark the strongest mark. Write one line about why you chose it.
13. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/045-mark-rough.kra.

## Check your work
- Does your chosen mark read at 100 px?
- Does it work in black on white and in white on black?
- Does it look better without a frame?

## Make it yours
- Refine a second mark for a different business.
- Ask someone to pick their favorite of your three.

## New words
- **refine** — to improve by small changes
- **reversed** — white on a dark background
- **distinctive** — easy to tell apart from others
