# 043. Study Old Trademarks

**You make:** ~/Art/Design/043-mark-study.kra (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 042
**Time:** 40 min
**Read:** A Book of American Trade-Marks (Sinel 1924), PDF pages 5–6,15–16; Krita Manual, Rectangle Tool; Krita Manual, Polygon Tool; Krita Manual, Ellipse Tool

## The idea
Joseph Sinel collected American trademarks in 1924. His short note says what makes a mark good. It must be simple, so it works even when small. It must be flexible, so it can be made in metal, cloth, or print, in color or in one tone. An odd shape sticks in the mind better than a plain circle or square. A picture can speak in any language.

> "Simplicity is the keynote of a good mark." (A Book of American Trade-Marks, PDF page 5)

## Look
In the Library app, look at the page images. Pages 5 and 6: read the note. Page 15: the lion and letter R, white on color. Page 16: the J and P made of boxes. Page 29: the coach in a frame. Page 36: the dog on black.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Make a new document 1800 x 1200 px at 150 ppi.
2. Draw a 2 by 2 grid of four boxes with the Rectangle Tool. Set Fill to Not filled.
3. In the Library app, open page image 15 of Sinel's book.
4. Box 1: redraw the Rogers mark with flat shapes. Use the Polygon Tool and Ellipse Tool in black.
   You should see a simple mark made of a few shapes.
5. Box 2: look at page image 16. Redraw the J and P mark with the Rectangle Tool.
6. Box 3: look at page image 29. Redraw the coach in black, with no frame.
7. Box 4: look at page image 36. Redraw the dog as a white shape on a black rectangle.
8. Make each redraw only 150 px wide in a corner of its box. This is the small test.
   You should see which marks still read when small.
9. Under each box, write three yes or no answers: simple, one color, shaped by its subject.
10. Pick your favorite mark. Write one line about why.
11. Press Ctrl + S. Save as ~/Art/Design/043-mark-study.kra.

## Check your work
- Does each redraw still read when small?
- Can you tell which mark depends on a frame?
- Can you say why your favorite mark works?

## Make it yours
- Find three logos at home or school. Redraw them in flat black.
- Pick a mark you dislike and say which rule it breaks.

## New words
- **trademark** — a mark that names the maker of a product
- **device** — another word for a mark or emblem
- **flexible** — able to work in many sizes and materials
