# 019. Make Your Own Wall Texture

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/My-Bedroom/Bedroom-Wall-Texture.png  (then add it to My-Bedroom.sh3d)
**Builds on:** lessons 004 and 018
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 165–166; Wood and Burbank, The Art of Interior Decoration, PDF page 332

## The idea
Texture is how a surface looks and feels. Parsons says we learn rough and smooth by touch, and then see it with our eyes. A room needs consistent variety: not all one texture, and not all different. Wood and Burbank add a rule for pattern: do not put a figured surface on another figured surface. Plain walls make the best background. So make a quiet texture and use it on one wall only.
"A burlap cloth looks rougher than an India silk; chiffon looks more flexible than taffeta" (Parsons, PDF page 165)

## Look
- PDF page 165, bottom: find the burlap and silk lines.
- PDF page 166, middle: find "Consistent variety."
- Wood and Burbank PDF page 332, top: find the rule about spotted surfaces.

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Choose File > New. Set 512 px by 512 px. Name it Bedroom-Wall-Texture.
2. Choose View > Wrap Around Mode. Now edges join like tiles.
3. Choose a base color. Fill the canvas with Edit > Fill with Foreground Color.
4. Pick a second color, only a little darker or lighter.
5. Choose the Straight Line Tool. Draw soft vertical stripes. Hold Shift to keep the line straight.
6. Draw a few stripes that cross the right edge. See them reappear on the left. This keeps the tile seamless.
7. Add small dots or short lines with a pencil brush preset. Keep them quiet.
8. Look at the tile. Is it plain enough to use as a background?
9. Choose File > Export… Save as PNG named Bedroom-Wall-Texture.png in the My-Bedroom folder.
10. Open My-Bedroom.sh3d. Double-click the wall behind the bed. Plan > Modify walls…
11. Find the texture setting for the inside side. Click the Import button. Follow the texture import wizard.
12. Click OK. Look at the 3D view. Click the Save home button.

You should see: a quiet pattern on one wall, plain walls elsewhere.

## Check your work
1. Does the texture repeat with no seam?
2. Is the pattern on one wall only?
3. Is the rest of the room plain?

## Make it yours
- Make a second texture, like linen or wood grain, for a headboard panel.
- Check your bed cover. Does it fight the wall pattern?

## New words
- **texture** — how a surface looks and feels.
- **seamless** — with no visible joins when repeated.
- **figured** — having a pattern.
