# 051. Make a Mood Board in Krita

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/Client-Project/Client-Mood-Board.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lessons 019, 021, and 049
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 386–387; Krita Manual, Transform Tool

## The idea
A mood board is one page that shows the feeling of a design. It has colors, textures, shapes, and words. It shows the client the feel of the room before the plan is final. Parsons says there is no reason for a wild and unrelated choice. Every item must belong to one idea. "There is no reason, however, for a wild and unrelated choice in pictures any more than in other decorative objects." (Parsons, PDF page 386)

## Look
- PDF page 386, middle: find the lines about the harmony of idea in the choices.
- PDF page 387, top: find how too many pictures together show bad taste.

## Steps
1. Read your Client-Brief.kra. Pick three mood words, for example: warm, lively, calm.
2. Open Krita. Choose File > New. Set 3000 px by 2000 px. Name it Client-Mood-Board.
3. Use the Rectangle tool. Draw a large panel for color. Fill five strips with a palette that fits the words.
4. Make three texture patches as in lesson 19: wood, cloth, and wall. Draw each in a square.
5. Use a pencil brush preset. Sketch three simple furniture shapes: a sofa, a table, and a lamp. Keep them small.
6. Open the bedroom image you made in lesson 027. Copy it. Paste it onto the board.
7. Use the Transform tool to scale it. Keep it as one picture. Parsons says one picture at a time.
8. Use the Text tool. Write the three mood words in large letters.
9. Write one line for each: how the room will show this word.
10. Arrange the pieces on a loose grid. Leave empty space. Move things until the board feels balanced.
11. Remove anything that does not fit the three words.
12. Choose File > Save As… Save as Client-Mood-Board.kra in the Client-Project folder.

You should see: a board with a palette, three textures, three sketches, a photo, and three words.

## Check your work
1. Does every item match one of the three words?
2. Is there a clear focal item on the board?
3. Is there empty space between items?

## Make it yours
- Make a second board with the opposite words. What changes?
- Show both boards to someone. Which one fits the client? Why?

## New words
- **mood board** — a page of colors, textures, and shapes that shows a feeling.
- **swatch** — a small sample of a color or a material.
- **reference** — a picture used as a guide.
