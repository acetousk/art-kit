# 059. Cover Page and Design Statement

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/Portfolio/Portfolio-Cover-and-Statement.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lessons 057 and 058
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 357–358

## The idea
A cover tells a reader who made the portfolio and what it is about. A design statement tells them what you believe. Parsons says beauty is the quality of harmonious relationships. There is no formula for it. But there are principles, and each person uses them in her own way. Your statement says which principles matter most to you, in your own words.
"Beauty is the quality of harmonious relationships." (Parsons, PDF page 358)

## Look
- PDF page 358, bottom: find "A formula to produce it does not exist."
- PDF page 358, bottom: find the list of principles: colour, line, form, texture, arrangement.
- PDF page 357, top: find the idea that each house expresses one person.

## Steps
1. Open Portfolio-Page-Template.kra. Choose File > Save As… Save it as Portfolio-Cover-and-Statement.kra.
2. Use the Text tool. Type the title: Interior Design Portfolio. Add your name and the year.
3. Pick your best photo. Open it, copy it, and paste it on the cover. Make it dominate.
4. Add a short line under the title: one phrase that names your style.
5. Make a second page, a new file with the same size. Name it Design-Statement.
6. Write the statement in five to eight short sentences.
7. Sentence 1: What a room must do first. Use the word function.
8. Sentence 2: How you test space for all people. Name two ADA numbers you use.
9. Sentence 3: How you choose color. Name the light and the scheme.
10. Sentence 4: Your favorite design idea from the course. Name it.
11. Sentence 5: What you want to learn next.
12. Read it aloud. Cut every word that is not needed.
13. Choose File > Save As… Save the cover as Portfolio-Cover-and-Statement.kra. Save the statement page as Design-Statement.kra.

You should see: a cover with a dominant picture, and a statement of five sentences.

## Check your work
1. Does the cover have a title, a name, and one main picture?
2. Does the statement name real numbers and real ideas?
3. Did you cut every extra word?

## Make it yours
- Write the statement again for a client, not a teacher. What changes?
- Ask someone to say your style in three words after they read it.

## New words
- **statement** — a short text about what you believe in design.
- **style** — the look and feeling that is yours.
- **principle** — a basic idea that guides your work.
