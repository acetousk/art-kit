# 033. Movement in an Entry Hall

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/Room-Studies/Room-Studies.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 031 and 032
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 145–146; Wood and Burbank, The Art of Interior Decoration, PDF page 310; Wharton and Codman, The Decoration of Houses, PDF page 209

## The idea
The eye follows lines. Parsons calls this movement. A straight stripe leads the eye to its end, and a curve leads it on a wander. In a room for rest, too much movement tires the eye. A hall is a passageway, so Wharton wants strong, simple lines there. Wood and Burbank give a plan for a narrow hall: a very narrow table, a stiff chair on each side, and one thing above.
"This tendency by which the eye is led from one point to another by a continuous line, or one nearly so, is called movement" (Parsons, PDF page 146)

## Look
- Parsons PDF page 145: find the stripe on the left and the curve on the right. The eye follows each one.
- Wood and Burbank PDF page 310: find the plan for a narrow hall with a console and two chairs.
- Wharton PDF page 209: find the hall that "reverts to its original use as a passageway."

## Steps
1. Open Room-Studies.sh3d. Click Create walls.
2. Start 3 ft right of the sitting room. Draw a hall 5 ft by 14 ft inside.
3. Add a door at each short end. Click Create rooms, double-click inside, and name it Entry Hall.
4. Add a console table in the middle of one long wall. Set its depth to 14 in.
5. Add a chair on each side of the console, at equal distances from the center.
6. Hang a mirror or picture above the console. Set its elevation so it clears the table.
7. Add a runner rug along the hall. Keep its sides straight with the walls.
8. Click Create dimensions. Measure the clear path past the console. It must be 36 in or more.
9. Add a text label: Movement — a straight line leads you in.
10. Choose 3D view > Virtual visit. Look down the hall from the door. Does your eye run straight?
11. Click the Save home button.

You should see: a hall with a rhythm: chair, table, chair, and a clear path.

## Check your work
1. Is the clear path at least 36 in wide?
2. Is the rug straight with the walls?
3. Does the eye move in one direction?

## Make it yours
- Add a second console and chairs further along. Does the rhythm help?
- Add a curved rug. What does the eye do now?

## New words
- **movement** — the path the eye follows through a design.
- **rhythm** — a pattern that repeats in a steady way.
- **console** — a narrow table that stands against a wall.
