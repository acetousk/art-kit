# 049. Write a Client Brief

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/Client-Project/Client-Brief.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lessons 002 and 040
**Time:** 30–45 min
**Read:** Wharton and Codman, The Decoration of Houses, PDF pages 57–58; de Wolfe, The House in Good Taste, PDF page 29

## The idea
A client brief is a one-page list of what the client needs. A designer writes it before drawing anything. Wharton says: before you decorate a room, ask what it is for. A room is not "a library." It is the library that suits this person. de Wolfe says she studies the people who will live in the house and their needs, like an actress studies a part. Your client can be a real person or the sample family below.
"it is essential to consider for what purpose the room is to be used" (Wharton and Codman, PDF page 57)

## Look
- Wharton PDF page 57, middle: find that individuality means being comfortable in your own way.
- Wharton PDF page 58, top: find how old habits make a room hard to use.
- de Wolfe PDF page 29, top: find "I study the people who are to live in this house."

## Sample client (use this if you have no real client)
- **Who:** the Hart family: Dad, Mum, Mia (11), Leo (7). Grandma Rosa visits and uses a wheelchair.
- **Room:** living room, 16 ft (east to west) by 20 ft (north to south), ceiling 8 ft 6 in.
- **Door:** one door, 36 in wide, in the south wall, 2 ft from the west corner. It swings into the room.
- **Windows:** two in the north wall, each 4 ft wide and 4 ft tall, sill 30 in. North light is cool and dim.
- **Jobs:** family movie nights for five, homework for two kids at a table, a reading corner, toy storage, guests.
- **Keep:** Grandma's wooden rocking chair, and a sofa 84 in by 36 in.
- **Likes:** blue, warm wood, plants. **Dislikes:** clutter and dark corners.
- **Limit:** no more than six new large pieces.

## Steps
1. Choose your client: a real person, or the Hart family above.
2. Open Krita. Choose File > New. Set 2000 px by 2800 px. Name it Client-Brief.
3. Use the Text tool. Write the heading: Client Brief — and the client name.
4. With a real client, ask: Who uses the room? What jobs? What do you love? What must stay?
5. Write these headings in a list: Who, Room, Jobs, Must keep, Likes, Dislikes, Light, Access, Limits.
6. Fill in each one. Keep each answer to one line.
7. Under Access, write: 36 in paths, 60 in turning space, 32 in door.
8. Write a Success test: five yes-or-no sentences. Example: Can five people watch a movie?
9. Choose the Straight Line Tool. Draw a rough plan of the room. Mark north with an arrow.
10. Read the brief back to the client. Fix what they say is wrong.
11. Choose File > Save As… Save as Client-Brief.kra in a new folder, Client-Project, in ~/Art/Interior Design.

You should see: a one-page brief with a success test and a rough sketch.

## Check your work
1. Does the brief say what the room is for?
2. Does it list the access needs?
3. Can you test your design against the success test?

## Make it yours
- Write a brief for your own bedroom, as if you were the client. Compare it with lesson 2.
- Interview a real person for five minutes. What did you not expect?

## New words
- **brief** — a short list of what a client needs.
- **client** — the person you design for.
- **access** — the ease with which a person can reach and use a space.
