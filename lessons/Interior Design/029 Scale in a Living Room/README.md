# 029. Scale in a Living Room

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/Room-Studies/Room-Studies.sh3d  (new file)
**Builds on:** lessons 011 and 016
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 155–156; de Wolfe, The House in Good Taste, PDF pages 29 and 33

## The idea
Scale means how big each thing is next to every other thing, and next to the room. Parsons says every object must have the same good scale relation to every other object and to the room itself. de Wolfe gives a picture: a huge stuffed leather chair in a tiny gold and cream room is out of scale. The reverse is also wrong: tiny chairs in a big hall. You will build one living room and break its scale on purpose. Then you will fix it.
"every object used in the room unit must have the same perfect scale relation to every other object used and to the room itself" (Parsons, PDF page 155)

## Look
- Parsons PDF page 155, bottom: find the list of chair sizes that must agree.
- de Wolfe PDF page 33, bottom: find the chair "out of proportion as the proverbial bull in the china-shop."

## Steps
1. Open Sweet Home 3D. Click the New home button.
2. Click Create walls. Draw a living room 15 ft by 20 ft inside. Press Enter to type lengths.
3. Add a door and two windows from the catalog.
4. Click Create rooms. Double-click inside. Name the room Living Room.
5. Add a sofa, two armchairs, a coffee table, a bookcase, and a rug. Group them.
6. Break the scale. Make each armchair 60% bigger in Furniture > Modify… Look at the room.
7. Break it the other way. Make each armchair 40% smaller than normal. Look again.
8. Fix it. Give each piece a size that suits the others and the room. Use real furniture as a guide.
9. Add three dimension lines: sofa length, chair width, and room width.
10. Add a text label: Scale — what I changed. Write one sentence.
11. Click the Save home button. Save as Room-Studies.sh3d in a new folder, Room-Studies, in ~/Art/Interior Design.

You should see: a living room where no piece looks too big or too small.

## Check your work
1. Did you try too big, too small, and right?
2. Can you say why the final sizes feel related?
3. Do the pieces still leave a 36 in path?

## Make it yours
- Swap the rug for one half the size. What does it do to the room?
- Make a very small living room, 9 ft by 10 ft. Which pieces must shrink?

## New words
- **scale** — the size of an object compared with others and with the room.
- **out of scale** — too big or too small for its place.
- **group** — a set of pieces placed together for one use.
