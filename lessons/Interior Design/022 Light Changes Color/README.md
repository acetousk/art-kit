# 022. Light Changes Color

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/My-Bedroom/Bedroom-Color-Schemes.kra  (a Light Test box)
**Builds on:** lesson 021
**Time:** 30–40 min
**Read:** Wheeler, Principles of Home Decoration, PDF pages 90–91 and 93; Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF page 50

## The idea
A color is not fixed. It changes with the light. Parsons says the object stays the same, but the light that reaches the eye changes. So choose a color by the light in the room first. Wheeler says a north room needs warm, bright color, like gold and warm red. A south room has strong sun and wants cool, light color, like blue and water-green.
"A north room will require warm and bright treatment, warm reds and golden browns, or pure gold colours." (Wheeler, PDF page 90)

## Look
- Wheeler PDF page 90, top: find the north room and the gold sash curtains.
- Wheeler PDF page 93, top: find the south rooms and their cool colors.
- Parsons PDF page 50, middle: find the five light conditions in which color changes.

## Steps
1. Find which way your bedroom window faces. Use a compass app, or see where the sun sets (west).
2. Write the direction. Does your room get direct sun, or is it shaded?
3. Open Bedroom-Color-Schemes.kra in Krita. Add a new box called Light Test with the Rectangle tool.
4. Fill two big squares with your circled wall colors: one warm, one cool.
5. Look at them at noon in your room, near the window. Then look in the evening with the lamp on.
6. Write what changes: lighter, darker, more green, more yellow.
7. Decide by Wheeler's rule: warm and bright for a north room, cool and light for a south room.
8. Open My-Bedroom.sh3d. Select the four walls. Choose Plan > Modify walls… Set the inside color to your pick.
9. Choose 3D view > Modify 3D view… Move the light brightness down, then up. See how the color shifts.
10. Write your decision in the Light Test box: direction, warm or cool, and why.
11. Save the Krita file. Click the Save home button in Sweet Home 3D.

You should see: a Light Test box with a decision, and walls in your pick.

## Check your work
1. Do you know which way your window faces?
2. Does your color match Wheeler's rule for that direction?
3. Did you test the color in two kinds of light?

## Make it yours
- Test a yellow wall in a sunny room. Wheeler says use a lemon yellow, not a gold. Why?
- Test the color under a lamp at night. What is the biggest change?

## New words
- **exposure** — the direction a room faces to the sun.
- **warm color** — a color in the red, orange, yellow family.
- **cool color** — a color in the blue, green, violet family.
