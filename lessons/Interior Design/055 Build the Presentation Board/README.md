# 055. Build the Presentation Board

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/Client-Project/Client-Presentation.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lessons 049 to 054
**Time:** 40–45 min
**Read:** Wood and Burbank, The Art of Interior Decoration, PDF page 333; Wharton and Codman, The Decoration of Houses, PDF page 338

## The idea
A presentation board shows a client the whole idea on one page. It has a title, the brief in a few words, the mood board, the palette, the plan, and the photos. Show only what helps. Wood and Burbank say that elaboration can hurt a design, but careful removal never can. Wharton says simplicity is the supreme excellence. So keep the board plain, with clear space and one idea on each part.
"Inappropriateness and elaboration can defeat artistic beauty" (Wood and Burbank, PDF page 333)

## Look
- Wood and Burbank PDF page 333, top: find the sentence about "intelligent elimination."
- Wharton PDF page 338, top: find "The supreme excellence is simplicity."

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Choose File > New. Set 4000 px by 2800 px. Name it Client-Presentation.
2. Use the Text tool. Write the title: Living Room for the Hart Family (or your client's name).
3. Under the title, write the brief in three lines: who, jobs, key limits.
4. Choose File > Open… Open Client-Mood-Board.kra. Copy the picture. Paste it into the board. Scale it with the Transform tool.
5. Do the same for Client-Palette-and-Materials.kra.
6. Paste Client-Photo-4-Top.png as the plan view. Add a label: Plan.
7. Paste the three room photos. Make the best one the largest. This is the focal point of the board.
8. Choose the Rectangle tool. Draw thin lines or boxes to group the parts. Keep the lines quiet.
9. Under each picture, write one line that says what it shows, and why it fits the brief.
10. Write a short box: Tests passed. List the turning space, the 36 in paths, the 32 in door.
11. Remove anything that does not help. Leave space.
12. Choose File > Save As… Save as Client-Presentation.kra in the Client-Project folder.

You should see: one clear board with a title, a plan, a palette, and three photos.

## Check your work
1. Is there one largest picture?
2. Does each picture have a short caption?
3. Is there space around every part?

## Make it yours
- Make a second board in a different layout. Which reads faster?
- Show the board to someone for ten seconds. What do they remember?

## New words
- **presentation board** — a page that shows the whole design to a client.
- **layout** — how parts sit on a page.
- **elimination** — careful removal of what is not needed.
