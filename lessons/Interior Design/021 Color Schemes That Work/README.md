# 021. Color Schemes That Work

**You make:** ~/Art/Interior Design/My-Bedroom/Bedroom-Color-Schemes.kra  (new file)
**Builds on:** lesson 020
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 87–89; Vanderpoel, Color Problems, PDF pages 95–97

## The idea
There are two main ways to make colors agree. The first is likeness, or analogy: use neighbors on the color wheel, like yellow, yellow-green, and green. The second is contrast: use opposites, called complements, like red and green. Parsons warns that full-strength opposites fight each other. To make a contrast scheme work, mute the colors, and keep the strong color for small areas. Vanderpoel tells you that Chevreul named these two groups first.
"The small areas of intense colour show best and are strongest in their emphasis against the more neutralized ones of the complementary colour." (Parsons, PDF page 89)

## Look
- PDF page 87: find the groups of related colors. Blue, blue-green, green, yellow-green is one group.
- PDF page 88: find "Nothing can be cruder, harsher, or more commonplace than a rug in red and green."
- Vanderpoel PDF page 96: find the headings "Harmonies of analogy" and "Harmonies of contrast."

## Steps
1. Open Krita. Choose File > New. Set 2400 px by 1600 px. Name it Bedroom-Color-Schemes.
2. Open your Bedroom-Color-Chart.kra. Keep it open in a tab, so you can sample colors from it.
3. Draw three big boxes with the Rectangle tool. Label them A, B, and C with the Text tool.
4. Box A, related colors: fill five strips with neighbors from the wheel, for example blue, blue-green, green.
5. Box B, contrast: fill one big strip with a muted green. Fill a small strip with a strong, pure red.
6. Box C, one color in many values: fill five strips with the same hue from light to dark.
7. Under each box, draw a tiny room: a wall, a floor, and a bed. Fill it with the scheme.
8. Look at each tiny room. Which feels calm? Which feels lively?
9. Write one line under each: what mood it makes.
10. Circle the scheme you will use for your bedroom. Write the reason.
11. Choose File > Save As… Save as Bedroom-Color-Schemes.kra in the My-Bedroom folder.

You should see: three schemes, three tiny rooms, and one circle.

## Check your work
1. Is your contrast scheme muted, with a small strong accent?
2. Does your related scheme use neighbors on the wheel?
3. Did you give a reason for your choice?

## Make it yours
- Make a fourth scheme from a color in a photo or a poster you love.
- Test your circled scheme on a bedspread, a rug, and a lamp. Does each belong?

## New words
- **analogous** — made of colors that are neighbors on the wheel.
- **complement** — the color opposite another on the wheel.
- **neutralize** — to mix a color with gray to make it quieter.
