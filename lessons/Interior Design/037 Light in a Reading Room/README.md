# 037. Light in a Reading Room

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/Room-Studies/Room-Studies.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 022 and 026
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Wood and Burbank, The Art of Interior Decoration, PDF pages 316–317; Wheeler, Principles of Home Decoration, PDF page 90; Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF page 357

## The idea
A reading room needs daylight and lamp light. Wood and Burbank say yellow and orange bring sunshine into a dark room. Use yellow with browns, greens, grays, or careful blues, not with reds. Wheeler says a north room needs warm, bright color. Parsons adds a test for any lamp: can you read by it? If not, it is in the wrong place.
"introducing plenty of yellow and orange you can bring sunshine into a dark living-room" (Wood and Burbank, PDF page 316)

## Look
- Wood and Burbank PDF page 316: find the sash curtains of thin silk in bright yellow.
- PDF page 317, top: find the colors that go with yellow.
- Parsons PDF page 357, bottom: find the lamps "so placed that it is impossible to read or sew by them."

## Steps
1. Open Room-Studies.sh3d. Click Create walls.
2. Start 3 ft below the sitting room. Draw a reading room 10 ft by 12 ft inside.
3. Add a door. Add one window on the north wall.
4. Click Create rooms, double-click inside, and name it Reading Room.
5. Set the walls to a warm, light yellow. Set the floor to warm brown and the ceiling to cream.
6. Add an armchair by the window, a small table, a bookcase, and a rug.
7. Add a lamp beside the chair. Place it so light falls over your shoulder onto the page.
8. Add a ceiling light in the middle.
9. Choose 3D view > Virtual visit. Lower the eyes elevation to seated height. Is the page lit well?
10. Click Create photo… Pick one of the two best quality levels. Make a photo at noon.
11. Make a second photo at evening. Save both PNGs in the Room-Studies folder.
12. Add a text label: Light — where the lamp stands and why.
13. Click the Save home button.

You should see: a warm room, a lamp by the chair, and two photos.

## Check your work
1. Is the lamp where you can read by it?
2. Are the warm colors used in a north room?
3. Do the two photos look different?

## Make it yours
- Put the lamp on the other side of the chair. Does the page get a shadow?
- Add thin yellow curtains. How does the window look?

## New words
- **sash curtain** — a thin curtain close against the glass.
- **north room** — a room that gets little direct sun.
- **task light** — a lamp placed to light one job.
