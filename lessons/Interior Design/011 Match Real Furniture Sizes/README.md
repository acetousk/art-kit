# 011. Match Real Furniture Sizes

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/My-Bedroom/My-Bedroom.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 009 and 010
**Time:** 30–40 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 155–156; Sweet Home 3D User's Guide, section "Adding doors, windows and furniture"

## The idea
A catalog bed is not your bed. To plan well, make each piece the same size as the real one. Parsons says the sizes of a chair must work together: height, width, depth, seat height, arms. Each size also has to fit its job. In Sweet Home 3D you set exact sizes in the furniture dialog.
"The proportion of height to width, and of each of these to the depth of the chair as a whole, must be considered." (Parsons, PDF page 155)

## Look
- PDF page 155, middle: find the list of chair sizes to study. Back, seat, seat height, arms.
- PDF page 156, top: find the idea that every part must fit the chair's job of comfort.

## Steps
1. Use a tape measure on your real bed. Write width, length, and height.
2. Measure your desk, chair, dresser or wardrobe, and nightstand the same way.
3. Open My-Bedroom.sh3d. Double-click the bed. You can also choose Furniture > Modify…
4. Type a name, for example Bed (mine).
5. Type the width, depth, and height you measured.
6. Click OK. Look at the plan. The piece changed size.
7. Repeat for each piece.
8. Open the home furniture list. Check that the sizes look right in the columns.
9. Move each piece to the place where it stands in your real room.
10. Compare the plan with the real room. Stand at the door and look. Do they match?
11. Click the Save home button.

You should see: every piece named and sized like your real furniture.

## Check your work
1. Does each piece have your measured size?
2. Does the plan look like the room you stand in?
3. Did you check the height as well as the floor size?

## Make it yours
- Add the bed you wish you had. Use its real size from a store note.
- Add a dimension line between the bed and the wall. How far is it?

## New words
- **proportion** — how the sizes of parts relate to each other.
- **depth** — the size of an object from front to back.
- **name field** — the text box that names a piece in the dialog.
