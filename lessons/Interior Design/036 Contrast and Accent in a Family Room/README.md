# 036. Contrast and Accent in a Family Room

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/Room-Studies/Room-Studies.sh3d
**Builds on:** lesson 035
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 88–89; Wood and Burbank, The Art of Interior Decoration, PDF page 317

## The idea
A contrast scheme uses opposite colors, like orange and blue. At full strength they fight. Parsons says to neutralize them with gray, and to use the strong color in small areas only. A family room is lively and can take contrast. Wood and Burbank give matches: gray walls go with dull blue or green curtains, and cream walls with yellow, brown, buff, or apple green.
"The small areas of intense colour show best and are strongest in their emphasis against the more neutralized ones of the complementary colour." (Parsons, PDF page 89)

## Look
- Parsons PDF page 88, top: find the red and green rug. Parsons calls it the crudest thing.
- Parsons PDF page 88, bottom: find "Full intense colours should not be brought near each other."
- Parsons PDF page 89, top: find the small areas of intense color.
- Wood and Burbank PDF page 317: find the list of wall and curtain matches.

## Steps
1. Open Room-Studies.sh3d. Click Create walls.
2. Draw a family room 15 ft by 18 ft inside, below the dining room. Add a door and two windows.
3. Click Create rooms, double-click inside, and name it Family Room.
4. Pick a pair of complements. Example: a muted blue-green and a warm orange.
5. Mix each with gray in Krita until both are quiet. Note their values.
6. Set the walls and the sofa to the muted blue-green. Use Plan > Modify walls… and Furniture > Modify…
7. Set the floor to a neutral wood.
8. Pick the strong orange for small things only: two pillows, one chair, a lamp.
9. Add a coffee table, a TV stand, a bookcase, and a rug.
10. Check the area. The strong color must cover far less than the quiet one.
11. Add a text label: Contrast — what is quiet and what is strong.
12. Click the Save home button.

You should see: a quiet room with a few strong notes.

## Check your work
1. Are both colors muted?
2. Is the strong color in small areas only?
3. Does the room feel lively but not loud?

## Make it yours
- Make the sofa the strong color and the walls quiet. What changes?
- Try a pair that Parsons says clashes at full strength. Then mute it.

## New words
- **intense** — strong and pure in color.
- **neutralized** — made quieter by mixing with gray.
- **accent** — a small strong spot in a quiet room.
