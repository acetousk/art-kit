# 053. Client Layout with Clearances

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/Client-Project/Client-Project.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 012 to 016, 032, and 050
**Time:** 40–45 min
**Read:** ADA 2010 Standards, PDF pages 111, 123–124, and 217; Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF page 143

## The idea
Now plan the furniture. Start with the brief and the jobs, then test the plan. The ADA says every room on an accessible route needs a turning space. Paths need 36 in. Doors need 32 in clear, with floor space to open them. Parsons says a group of five, with one in the middle and two on each side, gives rest.
"All rooms served by an accessible route shall provide a turning space complying with 304." (ADA, section 809.2.2, PDF page 217)

## Look
- ADA PDF page 217, top: find 809.2.2 and the advisory about a T-turn in a kitchen.
- ADA PDF page 124: find the door table again.
- Parsons PDF page 143: find the group of five objects.

## Steps
1. Open Client-Project.sh3d. Read your brief and your success test.
2. List the pieces: sofa, rocking chair, two chairs, coffee table, homework table and four chairs, bookcase, toy storage.
3. Place the sofa and chairs in a group facing a focal point: a media wall or a bookcase wall.
4. Place the rocking chair in the reading corner, near the north windows, for light.
5. Place the homework table where the daylight is best. Leave 36 in around it.
6. Place the bookcase and toy storage on walls, not in paths.
7. Double-click each piece. Set a name and the right size.
8. Add test pads: 60 in turning, 30 by 48 in beside a seat, and one at the door.
9. Click Create dimensions. Measure the main paths from the door. Each must be 36 in or more.
10. Move one piece at a time until every test passes.
11. Count the new large pieces. The limit is six.
12. Add a text label: Layout — key tests passed. Click the Save home button.

You should see: a furnished room with passed pads and a path from the door to each seat.

## Check your work
1. Does the 60 in turning pad fit?
2. Does each main path measure 36 in or more?
3. Is the number of new large pieces within the limit?

## Make it yours
- Make a second layout. Which one passes more tests?
- Ask a friend to walk each layout with a finger on the plan.

## New words
- **accessible route** — a path that anyone can use, including with a wheelchair.
- **parking space** — clear floor beside a seat where a wheelchair can stop.
- **layout** — the arrangement of furniture in a room.
