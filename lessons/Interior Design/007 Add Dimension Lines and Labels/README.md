# 007. Add Dimension Lines and Labels

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/My-Bedroom/My-Bedroom.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 004 to 006
**Time:** 25–35 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 165–166; Sweet Home 3D User's Guide, sections "Drawing dimensions" and "Adding texts"

## The idea
A plan with numbers is a working plan. A builder, a client, or you next month can read it. Parsons says you reach a good sense of scale only by "drawing, measuring or calculating." Dimension lines are how you do that in the plan. Labels tell the reader what each thing is.
"drawing, measuring or calculating in some way the exact relation of details" (Parsons, PDF page 165)

## Look
- PDF page 165, top: find "Constant care is necessary" for scale.
- PDF page 166, top: find how wood and cloth look different.

## Steps
1. Open My-Bedroom.sh3d. Click the Select button.
2. Click the Create dimensions button.
3. Click one end of the longest wall. Click the other end. Click a third time, away from the wall.
4. Point at a wall side or room side border. Double-click to accept the dimension. Click a third time.
5. Add a dimension for the second wall.
6. Add a dimension for the door width and one for each window width.
7. Click the Select button.
8. Click the Add texts button. Click in the room. Type the label Bed wall (or the name of a wall).
9. Use the text style buttons to make the text bold or larger. Select the text first.
10. Add labels for Door, Window, and Closet.
11. Check each number against your Krita sketch.
12. Click the Save home button.

You should see: dimension lines with numbers, and labels in clear spots.

## Check your work
1. Does every wall and opening have a number?
2. Can a stranger read the plan?
3. Do the numbers match your sketch?

## Make it yours
- Add a title text: "My Bedroom — floor plan". Make it big.
- Draw a dimension from the door to the window. Is the path wide enough?

## New words
- **scale** — the size of one thing compared to another.
- **label** — a short text that names a thing.
- **extension line** — a line that shows what the dimension measures.
