# 031. Balance in a Study

**You make:** add to ~/Art/Interior Design/Room-Studies/Room-Studies.sh3d
**Builds on:** lessons 029 and 030
**Time:** 35–45 min
**Read:** Parsons, Interior Decoration, PDF pages 124, 127–128, and 143–144; ADA 2010 Standards, PDF pages 114 and 223

## The idea
Parsons says balance is an equal pull of attractions. When a wall is balanced, the eye rests. In bisymmetric balance, one side mirrors the other across a center line. In occult balance, the two sides differ but pull the eye with equal strength. Size, color, shape, and texture all add pull. In a study the desk needs knee room, 27 in high at least, and a top 28 in to 34 in high.
"The term balance means a perfect equalization of attractions, whatever the attractions may be" (Parsons, PDF page 124)

## Look
- PDF page 127: find the nail on the wall. A nail on the right pulls the eye. A second nail on the left restores balance.
- PDF page 143, top: find the five-object group: one in the middle, two on each side.
- PDF page 144: find how a small bright thing balances a large dull one.

## Steps
1. Open Room-Studies.sh3d. Click Create walls.
2. Start 3 ft right of the dining room. Draw a study 10 ft by 12 ft inside.
3. Add a door and one window. Click Create rooms, double-click inside, and name it Study.
4. Pick the wall opposite the door. Draw a dimension along it. Find its center.
5. Put a desk at the center of that wall. Set its top to 30 in. Leave knee room under it.
6. Make it bisymmetric. Put a same-size bookcase on each side of the desk, equal distance from the center.
7. Add a text label: A. Bisymmetric balance.
8. Now work on a second wall. Put one big bookcase on the left end of it.
9. Balance it on the right with something small but strong: a bright chair or a tall lamp.
10. Color that piece in Furniture > Modify… Make its pull equal to the big bookcase.
11. Add a text label: B. Occult balance. Write what balances what.
12. Place a 30 in by 48 in test pad in front of the desk. It must be clear.
13. Click the Save home button.

You should see: one wall that mirrors and one wall that balances by weight.

## Check your work
1. Are the two sides of wall A equal?
2. Does the small object in B pull as hard as the big one?
3. Is there clear floor in front of the desk?

## Make it yours
- Group objects on a shelf in twos, threes, and fives, as Parsons says. Which group rests best?
- Make wall B too heavy on one side on purpose. See how it feels.

## New words
- **bisymmetric** — the same on both sides of a center line.
- **occult balance** — two unlike sides that pull the eye equally.
- **attraction** — anything that pulls the eye.
